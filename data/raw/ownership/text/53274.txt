CEO’S MESSAGE

Dear Stakeholder,

Sarvodaya Development Finance PLC’s performance for the first half of the financial year 2026/27 reflects continued
progress in building a financially resilient institution rooted in inclusive development. We delivered growth in income,
profitability and total assets while remaining focused on our purpose: enabling individuals, entrepreneurs and communities
to participate fully in Sri Lanka’s economic progress.

For the six months ended 30 September 2026, gross income increased by 39.0% year on year to LKR 3.99 billion, supported
by a 46.4% increase in interest income to LKR 3.81 billion. Net interest income grew by 29.1% to LKR 2.10 billion, while total
operating income increased by 20.3% to LKR 2.28 billion. Profit before taxation reached LKR 964.92 million, an increase of
24.5%, and profit after tax rose by 22.3% to LKR 416.47 million.

Our balance sheet continued to expand, with total assets reaching LKR 43.96 billion as at 30 September 2026, an increase
of 17.6% from 31 March 2026. Loans and receivables increased by 5.8% to LKR 21.81 billion, while lease rentals receivable
grew by 4.7% to LKR 9.62 billion. Amounts due to customers increased by 28.9% to LKR 13.67 billion, reinforcing the
importance of customer funding within our funding base. Total shareholders’ funds rose by 6.0% to LKR 4.69 billion, with net
asset value per share increasing to LKR 31.35 from LKR 29.57.

As our business grows, responsible lending and careful risk assessment remain essential. Impairment charges for loans
and other losses amounted to LKR 284.36 million for the six-month period, compared with LKR 242.16 million in the
corresponding period last year. Our focus remains on balancing portfolio growth with credit quality, supported by close
customer engagement and disciplined recovery practices.

Our development finance philosophy continues to guide our strategic direction. For SDF, long-term value lies in connecting
financial opportunity with productive enterprise, particularly within rural and agricultural communities. Supporting micro,
small and medium-sized businesses, women entrepreneurs and youth remain central to this approach. The relationships we
build and our understanding of customers’ circumstances are fundamental to delivering finance responsibly.

The opening of our new Kanthale branch reinforces this commitment by bringing our services closer to the communities
we seek to support. This expansion provides a stronger local presence from which to serve farmers, entrepreneurs and
households, while building the personal relationships that underpin our approach to development finance. Through the
branch, we aim to improve access to financial solutions that support livelihoods and enterprise development in the area.
Looking ahead, our priorities are to sustain profitable growth, manage funding and operating costs carefully, and safeguard
the strength of our balance sheet. Advancing digital capabilities and improving service delivery remain important to making
financial services more accessible and responsive. We will pursue these priorities with the same emphasis on accountability,
customer relationships and sustainable development that defines our institution.

I thank our Chairman and Board of Directors for their guidance, and our employees for their dedication and commitment. I
also extend my appreciation to our customers, depositors, shareholders, funding partners and regulators for their continued
support. Guided by the enduring principles of the Sarvodaya Movement, we remain committed to creating lasting value for
our stakeholders and the communities we serve.

Sincerely,




Nilantha Jayanetti
Chief Executive Officer

3. FINANCIAL REPORTING BY SEGMENT									
For management purposes, the Company is organised into business units based on its products and services and has three reportable
segments, as follows:													
							
Lease									
This segment includes Leasing products offered to the customers.									
														
Loan									
This segment includes SME, Micro, Personal and Gold loans offered to the customers.							
												
Other Business									
This segment includes all other business activities that the Company engaged other than above segments.					
												
The Management Committee monitors the operating results of its business units separately for the purpose of making decisions about
resource allocation and performance assessment. Segment performance is evaluated based on profit or loss and is measured consistently
with profit or loss in the financial statements. Also, the Company’s financing (including finance costs and finance income) and income taxes
are managed on a Company basis and are not allocated to operating segments.							
		
Transfer prices between operating segments are on an arm’s length basis in a manner similar to transactions with third parties.
