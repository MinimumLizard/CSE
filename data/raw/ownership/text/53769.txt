DFCC Bank Advances Core Business Growth and Expands Banking Franchise in Q3
2026


    •   Group Core Business Profit After Tax of LKR 7.6 Bn
    •   Group Total Capital Adequacy Ratio of 14.110%
    •   Group Net Fee and Commission Income up by 23% to LKR 6.5 Bn
    •   Group Total Assets up by 13% to LKR 966 Bn

DFCC Bank recorded continued growth across its core banking businesses during the nine
months ended 30 September 2026, supported by expanding lending and deposit portfolios,
stronger income generation and improving asset quality. The Bank's loan portfolio grew by
18% to LKR 610 Bn, while customer deposits increased by 23% to LKR 694 Bn compared
with 31 December 2025. Total assets expanded by 13% to LKR 965 Bn. Net Interest Income
increased by 8% to LKR 24.8 Bn, while Net Fee and Commission Income grew by 23% to LKR
6.5 Bn compared with the corresponding period of 2025.

Growth across these core banking businesses contributed to a Group Profit After Tax of LKR
7.6 Bn for the nine-month period. Although this was 12% below the corresponding period of
2025, the Bank recorded an 18% expansion in its loan portfolio, 23% growth in deposits and
a 31% increase in CASA balances compared with December 2025, while the net Stage 3
impaired loan ratio improved to 3.44% from 4.55%. These results demonstrate the
continued expansion of the Bank's franchise, with a clear focus on converting greater scale
and stronger income streams into sustainable profitability.

A major strategic milestone during the third quarter was the completion of DFCC Bank's
acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank PLC's Wealth and Retail Banking business in Sri
Lanka. The transaction broadened the Bank's customer base and strengthened its retail
banking, cards, deposits and wealth management capabilities. The Bank remains focused on
maintaining service continuity, building customer confidence and realising the opportunities
created by its enlarged franchise.

The Bank maintained a prudent approach to credit risk, strengthening impairment
provisions through refinements to its credit risk models and specific management overlays
in response to prevailing economic and geopolitical uncertainties. Impairment charges
increased by 10% compared with the corresponding period of 2025. Operating expenses
also reflected investments in technology, customer service and the expanded workforce
following the acquisition. These measures support the Bank's commitment to sound risk
management and disciplined, sustainable growth.
Alongside its expanded franchise, DFCC Bank continued to improve accessibility and
customer experience. During the quarter, the Bank introduced an ATM and Cash Recycler
Machine experience offering transactions in eight languages at Bandaranaike International
Airport and selected tourism destinations. It also continued to develop customer
propositions across cards, housing finance, pensioner banking and women's
entrepreneurship, including DFCC Aloka, while strengthening transaction banking and
trade-related services.

The Bank's progress was also reflected in external recognition across customer service,
transaction banking, people development and financial performance. These achievements,
together with its expanded capabilities, reinforce DFCC Bank's focus on deepening customer
relationships, maintaining financial discipline and translating business growth into long-
term value for customers, employees and shareholders.

This commentary relates to the unaudited financial statements for the period ended 30
September 2026, presented in accordance with Sri Lanka Accounting Standard 34 (LKAS 34)
on Interim Financial Statements.

INCOME STATEMENT ANALYSIS

PROFITABILITY

DFCC Bank PLC, the largest entity within the Group, reported a Profit Before Tax (PBT) of
LKR 8,736 Mn and a Profit After Tax (PAT) of LKR 7,323 Mn from core operations for the
period ended 30 September 2026, compared to a PBT of LKR 12,151 Mn and a PAT of LKR
8,328 Mn in the corresponding period of 2025.

At Group level, for the period ended 30 September 2026, PBT was LKR 9,130 Mn and PAT
was LKR 7,567 Mn, compared to LKR 12,480 Mn and LKR 8,530 Mn, respectively, in 2025.
The Bank’s Earnings Per Share (EPS) from core banking operations was LKR 16.38 for the
period ended 30 September 2026.

The Bank’s Return on Assets (ROA) before tax was 1.20%, while Return on Equity (ROE)
after tax stood at 8.68% for the period ended 30 September 2026.

The Bank’s total tax expense, including Value Added Tax (VAT) on financial services, Social
Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) on financial services, and Income Tax, amounted to LKR
4,727 Mn for the period ended 30 September 2026. Consequently, the Bank’s tax expense as
a percentage of operating profit stood at 39% for the period.
NET INTEREST INCOME

The Bank maintained its revised deposit and lending rates during the period in line with
prevailing market conditions and the Central Bank of Sri Lanka's Overnight Policy Rate.
Consequently, Net Interest Income increased by 8% to LKR 24,770 Mn, supported by
disciplined margin management, effective balance sheet optimisation, and continued growth
in earning assets. The Bank's asset base expanded by 13% over the past twelve months,
while the loan portfolio recorded 23% growth compared with 30 September 2025, reflecting
a strategic focus on quality asset expansion and sustainable business growth.

The Bank also strengthened its funding profile, with the CASA portfolio increasing by 31%
compared to 31 December 2025, resulting in the CASA ratio improving to 26.15% as at 30
September 2026. The higher contribution from CASA deposits supported funding cost
optimisation and contributed positively to Net Interest Income. Despite a competitive
interest rate environment and prevailing market dynamics, the Bank maintained a healthy
Net Interest Margin of 3.64%, underscoring the effectiveness of its prudent funding, pricing,
and balance sheet management strategies.


FEE AND COMMISSION INCOME

Strategic focus on trade-related commissions and card-based services continued to drive
strong growth in fee-based income, further enhanced by the successful integration of the SCB
retail banking portfolio, which expanded the Bank's credit card customer base and
transaction volumes. The growth in card-based activity and trade-related services
contributed significantly to the overall increase in fee income during the period.

While fee-related expenses increased in line with customer acquisition, portfolio expansion,
and higher transaction volumes, the overall impact remained positive. Consequently, Net Fee
and Commission Income increased by 23% to LKR 6,508 Mn, compared to LKR 5,281 Mn in
the corresponding period of 2025.


IMPAIRMENT CHARGE ON LOANS AND OTHER LOSSES

The net Stage 3 impaired loan ratio improved to 3.44% as at 30 September 2026, from 4.55%
as at 31 December 2025, supported by recoveries and portfolio expansion.

In response to current and potential future impacts of global and domestic economic
conditions on the Bank’s lending portfolio, management strengthened impairment
provisioning during the period. This was achieved through enhancements to internal
expected credit loss models to capture risk factors not fully observable in the current volatile
geopolitical and economic environment, including the recognition of additional provisions
as management overlays for exposures to higher-risk sectors and specific customer
segments, making additional provision for specific large group exposures.
The Bank also maintained adequate provisioning in line with the accelerated growth in its
lending portfolio. As a result, impairment charges increased to LKR 4,807 Mn for the period
ended 30 September 2026, compared to LKR 4,367 Mn recorded in the corresponding period
of 2025. These provisions have been established to safeguard the Bank’s financial strength
and reflect a more conservative assessment of potential credit risks, considering prevailing
macroeconomic conditions and the potential impact of the geopolitical environment.

OPERATING EXPENSES

Technology and digital transformation remained key strategic priorities for the Bank during
the period, supported by continued investments in IT infrastructure aimed at enhancing
digital capabilities, strengthening information security, improving operational efficiency,
and delivering a seamless customer experience across multiple channels. The Bank also
increased its investment in marketing and business development initiatives to strengthen
brand visibility, deepen customer engagement, and support growth across key business
segments. These investments are expected to generate long-term value by reinforcing the
Bank's market position, expanding its customer franchise, and enhancing its competitive
advantage in an evolving financial services landscape.

Operating expenses were further impacted by higher card-related costs associated with
increased transaction volumes and the expansion of card operations following the
integration of the SCB retail banking portfolio. In addition, personnel expenses increased due
to annual salary revisions, performance-based incentive payments, and the absorption of
employees as part of the acquisition, reflecting the Bank's commitment to supporting growth
while attracting, retaining, and rewarding talent.

As a result of these strategic growth initiatives and personnel-related costs, total operating
expenses for the period ended 30 September 2026 increased to LKR 16,906 Mn from LKR
13,348 Mn in the corresponding period of 2025. Notwithstanding these investments, the
Bank remains focused on maintaining prudent cost discipline and driving operational
efficiencies to support sustainable growth, profitability, and long-term shareholder value.

FINANCIAL POSITION ANALYSIS

ASSETS

Total assets increased by LKR 107 Bn, representing a 13% growth compared to 31 December
2025, primarily driven by the expansion of the Bank's loan portfolio and the successful
acquisition of the Wealth and Retail Banking Business of SCB. The loan book grew by LKR 94
Bn to LKR 610 Bn from LKR 516 Bn as at 31 December 2025, reflecting an 18% increase
during the period.
This performance highlights the successful execution of DFCC Bank's strategic growth
initiatives, combining strong organic business expansion with the benefits of the portfolio
acquisition, while maintaining a disciplined lending approach focused on asset quality and
sustainable growth. The continued recovery in economic activity and improving business
confidence further supported credit demand, reinforcing the Bank's role in facilitating
prudent credit expansion and contributing to national economic development.

LIABILITIES

The Bank’s total liabilities increased by LKR 105 Bn, reflecting a 14% growth from December
2025. The deposit base expanded by 23%, rising by LKR 129 Bn to LKR 694 Bn, up from LKR
565 Bn as at 31 December 2025, resulting in a loan-to-deposit ratio of 95.51%. Additionally,
the CASA ratio stood at 26.15% as at 30 September 2026.

The Bank effectively contained funding costs by utilising medium- to long-term
concessionary credit lines, which supported the expansion of the lending portfolio and
provided concessionary funding to customers. Factoring in these term borrowings, the CASA
ratio further improved to 29.95%, while the loan-to-deposit ratio improved to 90.56% as at
30 September 2026.


EQUITY AND COMPLIANCE WITH CAPITAL REQUIREMENTS

As at 30 September 2026, total equity was LKR 110 Bn, supported by a profit after tax of LKR
7.3 Bn and movements across the Bank’s securities portfolios.

In alignment with the Bank’s growth strategy and the improving economic environment, the
loan portfolio grew by 18%. Leveraging the strengthened equity base, the Bank effectively
absorbed the additional capital requirements associated with portfolio growth. The Tier 1
Capital Ratio was maintained at 10.437%, while the Total Capital Ratio stood at 14.017%,
compared to 13.550% and 15.933%, respectively, as at 31 December 2025.

The Bank’s Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) stood at 129.88%, and the Liquidity Coverage
Ratio (LCR) – all currency – stood at 169.46%, both comfortably exceeding regulatory
minimums.

DFCC Bank continued to grow its core businesses during the nine months ended 30
September 2026. Loans increased by 18% to LKR 610 Bn and deposits by 23% to LKR 694

against the corresponding period of 2025. Group Profit After Tax was LKR 7.6 Bn, 12% lower

end.
We strengthened impairment provisions through credit risk model refinements and
management overlays, while continuing to invest in people, technology and operating
capacity. Capital and liquidity remained above regulatory requirements. Our focus is to
convert greater scale and stronger customer relationships into sustainable earnings, with
disciplined judgement on risk and costs.
The acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank's Wealth and Retail Banking business brought
approximately 50,000 customer accounts and around 260 colleagues into DFCC Bank,
expanding our network and strengthening our retail and wealth capabilities. Completion
was an important milestone; the real test is whether customers continue to feel well served
and our new colleagues feel that they belong and can contribute. We are working to preserve
trusted customer relationships and build one stronger team. Our Great Place to Work
certification and recognition among Sri Lanka's Top 10 Mom-Inclusive Workplaces set a
standard we must make real in everyday experience. I thank everyone who has helped bring
the businesses together.
In July, DFCC Bank introduced Sri Lanka's first ATM and Cash Recycler Machine experience
offering transactions in eight languages at Bandaranaike International Airport and selected
tourism destinations. It reflects our belief that banking technology should adapt to
customers. We also continued to develop propositions for migrant workers through
Rataviru, pensioners through DFCC Garusaru, and women and entrepreneurs through DFCC
Aloka.
We strengthened transaction banking and international trade, and were named Sri Lanka's
Best Cash Management Bank for 2026 by The Asian Banker. Our Master Trade Loan
Agreement with the Export-Import Bank of India further supports trade between the two
countries. In September, we hosted the 49th ADFIAP Annual Meetings in Colombo, bringing
regional development finance leaders from twenty countries together to discuss investment,
climate finance, digital transformation, SME development and social inclusion.


Recognition during the period included two country awards at the ICC Emerging Asia
Banking Awards, two Silver and one Bronze award at SLIM DIGIS 2.6, and ADFIAP awards
for our Above and Beyond service culture transformation and the APark SME project. We
value this recognition, while remaining focused on the work and results behind it.
We will convert our increased scale into stronger relationships, better customer experiences
and sustainable performance, while managing credit quality, capital, liquidity and costs with
care. I thank our customers, colleagues, shareholders, regulators and partners for their
continued trust and support. We move forward with broader capabilities and a
responsibility to deepen our contribution to Sri Lanka.

Explanatory Notes
Note 1. Statement of Compliance

The consolidated financial statements of the Group and the separate financial statements of the Bank have been presented in accordance with the Sri
Lanka Accounting Standards 34 (LKAS 34) "Interim Financial Statements" issued by The Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri
Lanka) and in compliance with the requirements of the Companies Act, No. 07 of 2007 and the Banking Act No. 30 of 1988 and amendments thereto.

Note 2. Accounting Policies and Accounting Estimates
There were no changes to the accounting policies and method of computation since the publication of the last annual financial statements.

Note 3. Group Financial Statements

The Group Financial Statements comprise the Bank and its subsidiaries DFCC Consulting (Pvt) Limited, Lanka Industrial Estates Limited and Synapsys
Limited and group interest in associate company National Asset Management Limited. Investments in Subsidiaries and associate companies are
accounted under cost method in Bank’s financial statements. Investment in associate is accounted under equity method in group's financial statements.

Note 4. Quarterly Update on Sustainability-related Financial Disclosures
With effect from January 01, 2025, the Bank adopted the SLFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards, comprising SLFRS S1, General Requirements for
Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information, and SLFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures.

The Bank’s initial Sustainability-related Financial Disclosures, presented in the Annual Report 2025, were prepared applying the transition reliefs
permitted under SLFRS S1 and SLFRS S2 for first annual reporting period. The Bank identified and disclosed its climate-related risks and opportunities
(CRROs) in accordance with the requirements of SLFRS S2.

As part of the ongoing review process, management reassessed the climate-related risks and opportunities disclosed in the Annual Report 2025,
considering developments during the period ended 30 September 2026. Based on this reassessment, no material changes were identified that would
require an update to the climate-related financial disclosures previously reported for the year ended 31 December 2025

Note 5. Expected Credit Loss calculation

In response to the current and potential future impacts of global and domestic economic conditions on the Bank’s lending portfolio, management
strengthened impairment provisioning during the period. This was underpinned by enhancements to internal expected credit loss models designed to
better capture risk factors not fully observable in the prevailing volatile geopolitical and economic environment, including the application of
management overlays to recognize additional provisions for exposures to higher‑risk sectors and customer segments.

SLFRS 3 – Business Combinations Disclosure

(a) Acquisition Overview

On 1 August 2026, the Group acquired the Wealth and Retail Banking Business of Standard Chartered Bank Sri Lanka Branch (“SCB Sri Lanka”), comprising retail loans,
customer deposits, transferred employees, and branch lease rights. The acquired assets and activities meet the definition of a business under SLFRS 3. Effective control
transferred on 1 August 2026 ,and accordingly, the acquisition has been accounted for as a business combination in the interim financial statements as at 30 September 2026.
This transaction was previously disclosed in the financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026 as a non-adjusting event after the reporting date, as the acquisition
had not yet occurred and the related accounting had not commenced at that time.

(b) Purchase Price Allocation

The accounting for the acquisition remains provisional. The Group is currently identifying and valuing separately identifiable intangible assets acquired through the
transaction. Pending completion of this exercise, the excess of the consideration transferred over the provisional fair value of identifiable net assets acquired has been
recognised as provisional goodwill.
In accordance with SLFRS 3, the Group has a measurement period of up to 12 months from the acquisition date (up to 31 July 2027) to finalize the purchase price allocation.
No measurement period adjustments were recorded during the current reporting period. Pending completion of this exercise, the excess of the consideration transferred over
the provisional fair value of identifiable net assets acquired has been recognised provisionally as goodwill. The amount recognised as provisional goodwill may be adjusted
during the measurement period after the valuation of identifiable intangible assets and related deferred tax liabilities is finalised.


(c) Nature of the Acquisition
The acquired business primarily comprises the loan portfolio (including credit cards), deposits, the organised workforce and associated lease rights, which together constitute a
‘business’ as defined under SLFRS 3.

Fair Values of Financial Instruments

Financial Instruments Recorded at Fair Value
The following is a description of how fair values are determined for financial instruments that are recorded at fair value using valuation
techniques. These incorporate the Bank’s estimate of assumptions that a market participant would make when valuing the instruments.

(a) Derivatives
Derivative products valued with market-observable inputs are mainly currency swaps and forward foreign exchange contracts. Such
valuations incorporate various inputs such as foreign exchange spot and forward rates.

(b) Financial Assets at Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income
Financial Assets at Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income are valued using valuation techniques or pricing models primarily
consisted of unquoted equities.

(c) Financial Assets at Fair Value through Profit or Loss
Quoted Equities and Sri Lanka Government Securities - are included under Financial Assets at Fair Value through Profit or Loss are
valued using market prices.

Determination of Fair Value and Fair Value Hierarchy
The Bank uses the following hierarchy for determining and disclosing the fair value of financial instruments by valuation technique.

Level 1: Quoted (unadjusted) prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.

Level 2: Other techniques for which all inputs which have a significant effect on the recorded fair value are observable, either directly or
indirectly.

Level 3: Techniques which use inputs that have a significant effect on the recorded fair value that are not based on observable market
data.

Fair Value of Financial Assets and Liabilities not Carried at Fair Value
The following describes the methodologies and assumptions used to determine fair values for those financial instruments which are not
already recorded at fair value in the Financial Statements.
Assets for which Fair Value Approximates Carrying Value
For financial assets and financial liabilities that have a short term maturity, it is assumed that the carrying amounts approximate their fair
value. This assumption is applied for Cash and Cash Equivalents, Balances with Central Bank of Sri Lanka, Placements with Banks and
Securities Purchased under Re-sale Agreements. This assumption is also applied to demand deposits, savings accounts without a specific
maturity, floating rate instruments and fixed rate instruments having maturities within 12 months.

Fixed Rate Financial Instruments
The fair value of fixed rate financial assets and liabilities (other than assets and liabilities with maturities within 12 months) carried at
amortised cost are estimated by comparing market interest rates when they were first recognised with current market rates for similar
financial instruments.
