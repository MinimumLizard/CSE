FINANCIAL REVIEW
Income and Profitability
The Company reported consistent year-on-year top-line growth of 39% during the second quarter of 2026, while maintaining
a stable financial position and profitability. This performance was supported by effective management strategies and prudent
underwriting practices.

Gross Written Premium (GWP) reported for the six months ended 30 June 2026 was Rs. 5,207.84 Mn, compared to Rs.
3,752.80 Mn reported in the corresponding period of 2025, reflecting a growth of 39% for the first six months of the year.

Profit before tax for the second quarter of 2026 stood at Rs. 525.58 Mn, compared to Rs. 321.57 Mn recorded in the
corresponding period of 2025. Profit after tax for the period under review was Rs. 357.28 Mn, compared to Rs. 221.58 Mn
reported in the same period of the previous year. Accordingly, the Company’s overall profitability recorded a healthy growth
of 61% compared to the previous year.

The Motor segment remained the largest contributor to the Company’s Gross Written Premium (GWP), accounting for 78%
of the total portfolio and recording a strong growth of 39% compared to the previous financial year. The Fire segment also
demonstrated notable growth, increasing by 23% and contributing 13% to the overall GWP during the first six months of the
year.

The Company’s claims cost for the second quarter of 2026 amounted to Rs. 2,050.14 Mn, compared to Rs. 1,373.34 Mn
recorded in the corresponding period of 2025. Meanwhile, operating expenses increased by 12% to Rs. 1,109.46 Mn, up
from Rs. 994.24 Mn in 2025, in line with the Company’s business expansion strategy.

The Company’s underwriting loss reduced significantly by 60% to Rs. 92.44 Mn in 2026, compared to Rs. 231.90 Mn in
2025, driven by improved underwriting performance.
Investment and other income which comprises interest and dividend income, net fair value gains/ losses and other income,
increased by 12% to Rs. 618.02 Mn in 2026 reflecting the impact of higher investment returns.

Financial Position
Total shareholders' equity increased to Rs. 6,328.77 Mn in 2026, reflecting a 6% growth compared to Rs. 5,970.13 Mn in
2025.

Liquidity and Solvency
Cash equivalents increased by Rs. 93.38 Mn in 2026 Compared to previous year demonstrating a strong liquidity position
while optimizing investments for long-term growth.

The Company maintained its Capital Adequacy Ratio well above the regulatory minimum during the period, reflecting a
strong liquidity position and its continued ability to meet policyholder obligations with confidence.
The Company is committed in driving continues sustainable growth while fostering stakeholder expectations aligning with
strategic business objectives.

 4. Basis of preparation
   The interim financial statements of the Company for the quarter ended 30th June 2026 have been prepared in accordance
   with Sri Lanka Accounting Standard LKAS 34 – Interim Financial Reporting. These interim financial statements should be
   read in conjunction with the annual financial statements for the year ended 31st December 2025. Further, provisions of the
   Companies Act No. 7 of 2007 have been considered in preparing the interim condensed financial statements of the Company.
   The same accounting policies have been followed in preparation of these interim condensed financial statements as stated in
   the audited financial statements.

   In accordance with LKAS 34.19, these interim financial statements do not comply fully with Sri Lanka Accounting Standards
   (SLFRSs) as the Company has elected to apply the Statement of Alternative Treatment (SoAT) issued by the Institute of
   Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri Lanka) in respect of the initial adoption of SLFRS 17 Insurance Contracts for
   insurance entities.

   Accordingly, these interim financial statements are prepared on the alternative basis described below.
   Application of Statement of Alternative Treatment (SoAT) – Q2 Interim Financial Reporting
   SLFRS 17 Insurance Contracts is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 01st January 2026. The
   company has adopted SLFRS 9 prior to the implementation of SLFRS 17 and is currently preparing its financial statements in
   accordance with SLFRS 9 requirements. In response to implementation challenges affecting the insurance industry, CA Sri
   Lanka has permitted insurers to continue applying SLFRS 4 Insurance Contracts for the first and second interim reporting
   quarters of the initial year of adoption, subject to enhanced disclosures on the expected impact of SLFRS 17.


   The Company has elected to apply this relief for the quarter ended 30th June 2026.
   Accordingly, for this interim period:
   • Insurance contracts continue to be recognised, measured, and presented in accordance with SLFRS 4 Insurance Contracts.


   Progress of SLFRS 17 Implementation
   The Company has made substantive progress during the second quarter of the financial year towards full implementation of
   SLFRS 17, including:
   • Completion of major actuarial model builds for insurance contract measurement
   • Refinement of discount rate methodologies and risk adjustment frameworks
   • Parallel processing and dry runs of SLFRS 17 numbers
   • Strengthening of governance, controls, and data validation processes
   • Ongoing development of SLFRS 17-compliant accounting policies


   In line with the SoAT, the Company expects to:
   Provide full quantitative disclosures on all impacted line items and present provisional third quarter financial information
   prepared in accordance with SLFRS 17 in the Q3 interim financial statements.

   Expected Impact of SLFRS 17 – Q2 Update
   As required for the second interim reporting period, the Company provides below updated qualitative disclosures on the
   range of probable impacts of adopting SLFRS 17 on significant financial statement line items.

   Insurance Contract Liabilities
   • Insurance contract liabilities are expected to undergo material changes due to:
   - Measurement based on present value of fulfilment cash flows
   - Explicit risk adjustment for non financial risk
   • Certain portfolios, particularly short duration non life contracts, are expected to show reduced volatility, while longer
   coverage contracts may exhibit changes in liability profiles.

  Measurement of Insurance Contracts
  As at 30 June 2026, the company has assessed its product portfolio and elected to apply the Premium Allocation Approach (PAA)
  for the measurement of its insurance contracts, where applicable. Accordingly, for financial reporting purposes, insurance-related
  line items are recognized and measured under the PAA, as it provided a simplified and appropriate representations of the
  Company’s short-duration contracts in line with SLFRS 17 - Insurance Contracts.

  Insurance Revenue
  Insurance revenue is recognized in profit or loss over the coverage period in a manner that reflect the transfer of insurance services
  to policyholders. Under the Premium Allocation Approach, revenue is generally recognized on a time-apportioned basis, unless the
  expected pattern of risk release indicates a more appropriate systematic basis.

  Liability for Remaining Coverage (LFRC)
  At initial recognition, the Liability for Remaining Coverage is measured as the premium received (or receivable), adjusted for
  insurance acquisition cash flows and any amount arising from onerous contracts. Subsequently, the LFRC is reduced as insurance
  is recognized over the over the coverage period, with adjustments made for any change in estimates where applicable.


  Liability for Incurred Claims (LIC)
  The Liability for Incurred Claims represent the present value of fulfilment cash flows relating to claims incurred as at the reporting
  date. This includes reported claims and claims incurred but not reported (IBNR), together with an explicit risk adjustment for non-
  financial risk, determined in accordance with the principles of SLFRS 17.

  Insurance Acquisition Cash Flows
  Insurance acquisition cash flows that are directly attributable to portfolio of Insurance contracts are recognized as part of the
  Liability for Remaining Coverage. Such costs are systematically allocated to the profit or loss over the coverage period in line with
  the recognition of insurance revenue.

  Onerous Contracts / Loss Component
  Where facts and circumstances indicate that a group of insurance contracts is onerous at initial recognition or subsequently, a loss
  component is established within the Liability for Remaining Coverage. The loss component is recognized immediately in Income
  Statement and is subsequently adjusted for changes in fulfilment cash flows relating to future service.


  Reinsurance Contracts Held
  Reinsurance contracts held are measured separately under the Premium Allocation Approach, where applicable. The carrying
  amounts reflects premiums paid, recoveries on incurred claims and allowance for expected credit losses. Any net gain on
  reinsurance held that relates to onerous underlying contracts is recognized in Income Statement in accordance with SLFRS 17
  requirements.
