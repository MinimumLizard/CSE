Group Chief Executive Officers Review - Interim Financial Statements
Dear stakeholder,

I am pleased to present the Quarterly Financial Review of Capital Alliance Holdings PLC (CALH) for
the period ended 31st December 2025.

The Group reported a net profit of LKR 64mn for the quarter. This quarter’s profit was impacted by a
withholding tax (WHT) charge of LKR 510mn on dividends of LKR 3.95 bn declared by our subsidiary,
Capital Alliance Plc (CALT). Although recorded as an expense at group level, the WHT paid effectively
becomes a tax credit to shareholders of CALH on future dividends.
Excluding WHT, our profit from business activities stood at LKR 574mn for the quarter.

The CAL Group engages in four main business lines across Sri Lanka and Bangladesh: primary dealing,
stockbroking, asset management and investment banking. Our revenue broadly falls into two categories:
proprietary trading and fee-based income.

Although lucrative over the long term, revenue from proprietary trading is volatile on a quarterly basis.
CAL’s trading gains are mainly derived from Government Securities (where we profit when yields fall),
and listed equities. In Q1 of this year, CAL recorded trading gains of LKR 2.5bn as Treasury Bond yields
in Sri Lanka declined by 70 basis points (bps) on average, and the ASPI grew by 14%. Q2 trading
revenue stood at LKR 1.1 bn, where we capitalised on interim volatility to make profits on bond trading
despite a 10bps increase in rates, while we were aided by a 21% increase in the ASPI. The quarter under
review, Q3 saw relative stability, as interest rates increasing by 5bps and the ASPI growing by 4%. The
stability reduced opportunity for trading, and resulted in trading gains of LKR 856mn for the quarter. As
of the date of writing this letter (10th February), interest rates have declined by 15bps, while ASPI has
grown by 4.5% for the period since 31st December.

Our fee-based revenue has shown steady growth over the years. For the quarter under review, fee revenue
was recorded at LKR 817mn. YoY fee-based revenue for the year to 31st December has recorded an
increase of 31%. Over the past 5 years, fee-based revenue for the CAL Group has grown by 350% at a
CAGR of 46%. Fee based revenue has lower volatility. As it grows as a share of CAL’s overall revenue,
we will see lower variance in CAL’s group results over time.

All our business lines have performed well for the first three quarters of FY26. Highlights include CAL
acting as advisors on the landmark acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank’s personal banking business
by DFCC, the assets under management (AUM) of our asset management business surpassing USD 1bn,
and our digital customer base surpassing 50,000 clients.

During the quarter under review, we declared a dividend of LKR 1.00 per share-with our shareholders
receiving the dividend free of any WHT on account of WHT deductions on dividends received from
subsidiaries in prior years. Looking ahead, we anticipate a strong end to FY26 with a good final quarter,
driven by an impressive performance across all our divisions.

(Signed)

      Accounting classification and fair value of financial instruments
      Financial instruments measured subsequently on the ongoing basis either at fair value or amortized cost. The summary of significant accounting
      policies describes how the classes of financial instruments are measured, and how income and expenses, including fair value gains and losses, are
      recognized.
      The group measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of inputs used in making the measurement.

      Level - 1
      Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial instrument is regarded as
      quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those prices represent actual and regularly
      occurring market transactions on an arm’s length basis.

      Level - 2
      Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or accessible for these
      securities, their fair value measures are determined using relevant information generated by market transactions involving comparable securities.

      Level - 3
      Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique includes inputs not
      based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument’s valuation. This category includes instruments that
      are valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant unobservable adjustments or assumptions are required to reflect differences
      between the instruments.
