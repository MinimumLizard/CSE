5.   Going Concern Basis of Accounting

     The Directors have assessed the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern and are satisfied that the Group possesses the
     requisite resources to maintain its business operations for the foreseeable future. Directors confirmed that there is no
     intention to liquidate or cease operations of any business unit within the Group. In order to assess the Group’s ability, the
     Directors assessed the Subsidiary’s ability as a going concern since Muller and Phipps (Ceylon) PLC operates as a holding
     entity, maintaining its investment in its sole subsidiary, Muller and Phipps (Healthcare) Limited, and currently has no direct
     commercial operations.

     The Directors have assessed the subsidiary’s ability to continue as a going concern. Based on this assessment, they are
     satisfied that the subsidiary has adequate resources to continue its operations for the foreseeable future. In performing this
     assessment, the Directors considered that the subsidiary incurred a net loss after tax of Rs. 49,388,924/- for the year ended
     31 March 2026 (2024/25 – Rs. 60,444,335), has accumulated losses of Rs. 302,133,942/- as at 31 March 2026 (2025 – Rs.
     250,625,122), and reported net cash outflows from operating activities of Rs. 62,001,704/- during the year (2024/25 –
     positive operating cash flows of Rs. 20,502,276). Further, as at the reporting date, the subsidiary’s net assets are less than half
     of the stated capital and faces a serious loss of capital situation. These matters indicate the existence of a material uncertainty
     related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the subsidiary’s ability to continue as a going concern.

     However, the Directors also considered several mitigating factors. As at 31 March 2026, the subsidiary’s current assets
     exceeded its current liabilities by Rs. 207,854,713/- (2025 – Rs. 240,613,774), indicating that the subsidiary is in a positive
     working capital position and is expected to meet its obligations as they fall due in the normal course of business. In addition,
     the subsidiary has unutilized trust receipt facilities available to support the financing of inventory purchases, if required.

     The Management of subsidiary has also undertaken strategic initiatives to strengthen the it’s operational performance and
     future revenue streams. These include the launch of a new product range in February 2026 and the execution of 17
     partnership agreements. Out of these, 8 portfolios comprising 16 products have been submitted to the National Medicines
     Regulatory Authority (NMRA) for approval and are expected to be commercialized during the first half of the 2027/28
     financial year. Additional partnership agreements are currently under negotiation, which are expected to further enhance the
     subsidiary’s product portfolio and revenue-generating capacity. In addition, the Board of Directors of the subsidiary confirms
     that they do not have any intention to liquidate the Company or cease the operations at least 12 months from the date of
     signing the financial statements.

     Accordingly, based on the foregoing, the Directors of the Parent Company are therefore satisfied that the use of the going
     concern basis of accounting is appropriate and firmly believe that the Group will continue to operate as a going concern for
     the foreseeable future. Consequently, these financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis.
