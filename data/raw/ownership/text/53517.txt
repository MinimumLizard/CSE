LANKA REALTY INVESTMENTS PLC (ASCO.N)
Quarterly Financial Performance Review | 30 September 2026
Lanka Realty Investments PLC reported a strong year-on-year earnings recovery in Q2 FY2026/27, with Group revenue rising 154.9% to
LKR 416.00 Mn and profit after tax reaching LKR 146.65 Mn, against a loss of LKR 52.59 Mn in the prior-year quarter. The Group's
commercial property platform continues to anchor recurring revenue, complemented by a larger cash balance and a diversified portfolio
of bank fixed deposits and other investments. The LKR 166.85 Mn exceptional Mulberry finance cost reversal is separately identified to
distinguish one off gains from recurring earnings.

Financial Performance and Investment Position
Group revenue increased 154.9% to LKR 416.00 Mn from LKR 163.23 Mn in Q2 FY2025/26, while gross profit increased 132.5% to LKR
245.04 Mn. Operating profit was LKR 66.53 Mn, compared with LKR 24.43 Mn a year ago. Commercial-property operations generated
segment revenue of LKR 274.95 Mn and segment operating profit of LKR 194.12 Mn before Group eliminations, highlighting the role of
rental and investment-property operations in the Group portfolio. Gross margin was 58.9% versus 64.6% in the comparable quarter.
Profit before tax improved to LKR 186.11 Mn from a loss of LKR 26.50 Mn, with profit after tax of LKR 146.65 Mn versus a loss of LKR
52.59 Mn. Group earnings per share recovered to LKR 0.61 from negative LKR 0.41. For the six months ended 30 September 2026,
revenue was LKR 721.63 Mn (prior year: LKR 455.80 Mn) and profit after tax was LKR 195.07 Mn (prior year: loss of LKR 125.57 Mn). The
exceptional finance credit arising from the Mulberry settlement materially supports the Q2 profit outcome and should not be
extrapolated as recurring earnings.
Alongside its real estate portfolio, the Group reported LKR 1,583.98 Mn of bank fixed deposits as at 30 September 2026 (31 March 2026:
LKR 1,466.68 Mn; +8.0%). Management identifies LKR 1,374.47 Mn of non-current financial assets at amortised cost and LKR 209.51 Mn
of current financial assets at amortised cost as bank fixed deposits. Separately, other current assets increased to LKR 576.30 Mn from
LKR 133.06 Mn, a balance that management confirms includes unit trust holdings and short-term investments, alongside other items not
separately analysed in the interim financial statements. Group cash plus the separately classified bank fixed deposits totalled LKR
2,701.71 Mn; this gross measure spans different maturities and does not represent unrestricted cash.

Second Quarter Performance and Quality of Earnings
The reporting-period improvement reflects increased Group revenue, commercial-property earnings and the settlement of a legacy
financing obligation at Mulberry. A positive LKR 166.85 Mn credit in residential property finance expenses was reported in the segment
disclosure. Group net finance income was LKR 115.66 Mn, compared with a net expense of LKR 50.93 Mn in Q2 FY2025/26; these
movements include the exceptional credit and should not be interpreted as a recurring reduction in interest expense.
Excluding the LKR 166.85 Mn one-off credit on a simple, pre-tax basis would result in approximately LKR 19.26 Mn of profit before tax,
versus the reported LKR 186.11 Mn. This is an illustrative, non-statutory figure without consequential tax or non-controlling interest
adjustments, not a substitute for the interim financial statements.

Treasury Management, Debt and Liquidity
Lanka Realty Investments PLC continued to strengthen its financial resources during the quarter through disciplined treasury
management, active cash-flow planning and an emphasis on securing attractive returns on surplus funds. The Group's treasury strategy
balances readily available cash, fixed deposit investments and carefully managed investment maturities, supporting ongoing operations
and creating flexibility to evaluate high quality, income generating property opportunities. The Company remains focused on capital
preservation, funding efficiency and sustainable long-term value creation for shareholders.
As at 30 September 2026, the Group's cash in hand and at bank had increased substantially to LKR 1,117.73 Mn, compared with LKR
164.83 Mn as at 31 March 2026, representing growth of 578.1%. In addition, current bank fixed deposits amounted to LKR 209.51 Mn,
taking gross cash and current fixed deposit holdings to LKR 1,327.23 Mn. The Group also maintained LKR 1,374.47 Mn in non current
bank fixed deposits, bringing total fixed deposits to LKR 1,583.98 Mn. Consequently, gross cash and fixed-deposit holdings stood at LKR
2,701.71 Mn, demonstrating the scale and breadth of the Group's financial asset base. These figures represent gross balances; the
availability of individual deposits depends on their maturity and other terms.
The Group's total current assets increased to LKR 2,976.71 Mn from LKR 1,471.25 Mn at the preceding year end. Other current assets of
LKR 576.30 Mn include unit trusts and short-term investments, contributing to a diversified treasury investment portfolio, although the
investment portion is not separately disclosed in the interim statements. At the standalone Company level, cash and bank balances
reached LKR 924.42 Mn, with positive net cash and cash equivalents of LKR 919.09 Mn. Alongside this enhanced financial resource base,
LRI PLC continues to manage its LKR 4,500.20 Mn of interest bearing borrowings (excluding overdrafts) with a focus on borrowing costs,
maturity alignment and the cash yields generated by income producing properties. The full settlement of Mulberry's project specific
bank facility and the negotiated LKR 166.85 Mn finance cost reversal mark meaningful progress in resolving legacy obligations. The
Group intends to maintain a prudent financing structure, optimise treasury returns and allocate capital selectively to investments with
attractive risk-adjusted income and long-term appreciation potential.

Balance Sheet, NAV and Capital Allocation
At 30 September 2026, Group assets totalled LKR 28.66 Bn (March 2026: LKR 29.61 Bn), including LKR 21.24 Bn of investment property.
Bank fixed deposits of LKR 1,583.98 Mn, together with LKR 576.30 Mn in other current assets that include unit trusts and short-term
investments, broaden the Group's financial-resource base. These separately reported asset categories should not be equated with cash
equivalents or double counted. Equity attributable to the parent was LKR 10.24 Bn, with reported NAV per share of LKR 48.47 (March
2026: LKR 50.35). The movement in reported NAV per share principally reflects the Group's evolving capital structure following the
acquisition of Lee Hedges PLC and the preference-share financing undertaken through Lanka Realty Developments Pvt Ltd (LRD).

Corporate Finance and Portfolio Reorganisation - On’ally
The direct holding in On’ally Holdings PLC was transferred to Lee Hedges PLC within the Group. The September interim statements
record an LKR 788.72 Mn gain within the standalone Company’s Q2 profit or loss; this gain is eliminated on consolidation. The Company
reported Q2 profit after tax of LKR 959.41 Mn, which is therefore not a proxy for Group recurring earnings. The restructuring places
On’ally alongside the Lee Hedges / LRD commercial-property platform to support coordinated asset management and capital allocation.

Commercial Property - Recurring Earnings and Asset Management
The Group’s commercial-property portfolio is central to its strategy of strengthening recurring income and long-term asset values. Lee
Hedges PLC, Lanka Realty Developments (LRD) and On’ally Holdings PLC provide exposure to strategically located Colombo office, retail
and other commercial income-producing properties. The Q2 segment disclosure records commercial-property revenue of LKR 274.95 Mn
and operating profit of LKR 194.12 Mn before Group eliminations. This underscores the contribution of active property operations to
Group earnings, while the segment should not be equated with the consolidated operating result. Management is prioritising tenant
retention, occupancy and rent reviews, service-charge recoveries, collection efficiency, energy and maintenance-cost control, and
carefully selected improvements that raise sustainable net operating income.

The 30 September 2026 investment-property note identifies Lee Hedges PLC group (LRD and LHI) at LKR 7.65 Bn, On’ally at LKR 6.44 Bn,
Oak Street at LKR 4.96 Bn, Baseline Holdings at LKR 1.44 Bn and Crown Resort at LKR 0.74 Bn. These are disclosed carrying values, not
newly established market valuations. The Group aims to enhance value through active leasing, asset repositioning, redevelopment
optionality and disciplined capital expenditure, while evaluating acquisition opportunities with resilient tenants, attractive net rental
yields and debt-service coverage. Any claimed uplift in land or investment-property value requires appropriate independent valuation
support.

Colombo Land Bank, Baseline and Residential Value Potential
LRI PLC’s established land-bank and redevelopment holdings in and around the Colombo metropolitan commercial corridors provide
strategic exposure to land scarcity, connectivity improvements and future urban regeneration. Oak Street / 285 Darley Road and other
strategically positioned sites offer potential for higher-value use, phased development, joint ventures, or selective capital recycling,
subject to title, litigation, planning approvals, project feasibility and funding capacity. Preserving well-located land while managing
holding costs can create long-term optionality; however, prospective land appreciation is a strategic opportunity rather than realised
accounting profit. Independent appraisals and supported market comparables will be needed to substantiate any future uplift.
The Baseline property exposure adds a residential and land-value dimension to the portfolio. Management expects Colombo’s
established residential corridors to benefit over time from demand for accessible apartment locations and better-quality housing,
creating potential for improved selling prices and development margins. Such outcomes are dependent on prevailing buyer affordability,
transaction volumes, construction and financing costs, and project-specific approvals. Management will monitor observable apartment
sales prices, absorption and land comparables to guide the timing of any development, marketing or disposal decision; no unverified
appreciation gain is assumed in this review.
Leisure Portfolio and Diversified Earnings
The Group’s leisure exposure through the Weligama and Ahangama properties complements its core commercial-rental platform and
provides a route to tourism-led revenue diversification. The Q2 leisure segment reported revenue of LKR 38.94 Mn before Group
eliminations and a loss before tax of LKR 25.61 Mn. The next operating priority is to convert seasonal tourist demand into stronger gross
operating profit by lifting occupancy and achieved room rates, increasing direct bookings, controlling food-and-beverage cost, improving
digital distribution, and completing critical property maintenance ahead of peak trading.

Winter 2026/27 Tourism Season and Air Connectivity
Sri Lanka recorded 168,625 tourist arrivals in October 2026 (+2.1% year-on-year; +6.3% month-on-month), taking January-October
arrivals to approximately 1.86 million. The rise signals stronger visitor momentum ahead of the winter season, though arrivals do not
automatically translate into room yield or tourism receipts. India, the UK, China, Australia and Germany were prominent October source
markets. This supports the Weligama and Ahangama outlook, subject to effective marketing, room-rate discipline and conversion into
paid bookings.

International access is strengthening. On 6 November 2026, nearly 550 Polish visitors arrived via three Enter Air charter flights; 42 flights
were scheduled through 2 April 2027. Announced winter routes include Azur Air to Mattala, with further planned services or capacity
from S7 Airlines, French Bee, Centrum Air and Edelweiss. Management can target European and other inbound markets through surf
packages, airport-transfer tie-ups, direct-booking campaigns and travel-agent partnerships. Planned airline services and passenger
demand remain subject to change.


South Coast Surfing, Guest Experience and Leisure Earnings Potential
Weligama, Midigama and Ahangama are positioned for the south-coast surfing season, normally November-April, with relatively
consistent conditions December-March. Weligama is particularly attractive to novice surfers, while Ahangama and nearby reef breaks
draw more experienced travellers. Longer-stay surfing, wellness, excursions and food-and-beverage packages can increase guest
expenditure and diversify revenue beyond room sales.
A winter-season plan should combine dynamic pricing, international surf marketing, direct-booking conversion, timely preventive
maintenance and cost control. Weekly monitoring of occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, gross operating profit and collections will be essential to
achieving sustained positive leisure earnings. Tourist-arrival and airline information is post-30 September 2026 outlook context, not a Q2
realised benefit.


Overall Financial Positioning and Outlook
Q2 FY2026/27 reflects strong growth in reported revenue and a return to profitability, supported by commercial-property operations,
active asset management and the resolution of Mulberry's legacy financing exposure. The Group's cash and bank balances increased to
LKR 1,117.73 Mn, while current and non-current bank fixed deposits totalled LKR 1,583.98 Mn. Other current assets of LKR 576.30 Mn
additionally include unit trusts and short-term investments, although the portion attributable to those investments is not separately
disclosed. This strengthening of the Group's gross financial-resource base is a constructive indicator for shareholders and other
stakeholders, providing a foundation for disciplined treasury management and consideration of value-accretive opportunities. The Group
will continue to pursue sustainable commercial-property earnings, active management of Colombo land-bank and Baseline assets, and
improved leisure-sector performance, alongside selective acquisitions assessed against investment returns, funding requirements and
available liquidity. The LKR 166.85 Mn Mulberry finance-cost benefit is separately presented as an exceptional item.
