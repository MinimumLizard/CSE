Managers’ Review
For the period ended 30th September 2026
The Sri Lankan economy has experienced a growth of 4.2% for the second quarter of 2026. The country faces
numerous challenges resulting from geopolitical conflicts, global debt concerns and inflation. The Middle
Eastern conflict has resulted in increased commodity prices, particularly oil, thereby straining Sri Lanka’s
external sector. Rising fuel costs and commodities have resulted in headline inflation as measured by the CCPI
remaining elevated at 8.0% on a YoY basis. In anticipation of inflationary pressures, the Monetary Policy Board
of the Central Bank increased overnight policy rates by 100 basis points during the quarter under review. This
resulted in overall interest rates increasing with the 364-day Treasury Bill yield recorded at 9.95% by September
2026.
Guardian group reported a consolidated profit after tax of Rs. 0.43 billion for the first half of FY 2026 compared
to Rs. 3.90 billion for the same period in FY 2025. The reason for the profit decline is due to lower growth in
the investment portfolio stemming from an overall equity market decline. The All Share Price Index declined
by 1.20% for the six months compared to a growth of 37.71% for the same time period in the prior financial
year.
The active portfolio strategy is derived from our equity philosophy of investing in companies that display strong
fundamentals that lead to long-term potential for future value creation. During the quarter under review, we
purchased shares in Cargills Ceylon PLC and CT Holdings PLC to the amount of Rs. 12.05 Bn with funds from
our Active portfolio and re-classified this component under the strategic portfolio. As a result, the Active
portfolio size was reduced to Rs. 12.2 Bn by end of Sept 2026 and adjusted for this cashflow movement, its
performance was 0.83%. The total portfolio, which includes the strategic holdings, declined by 0.16%.

Financial Performance

Ceylon Guardian Investment Trust PLC reported a consolidated profit after tax of Rs. 428 million for the period
ending September 30, 2026, compared to the previous year's Rs. 3.90 billion. The financial performance was
largely affected by a decline in fair value gain of Rs.19 million from fair value through profit or loss of financial
assets due to the decline in the equity market, along with interest income of Rs.765 million, dividends of Rs.
227 million and net gain from disposal of fair value through profit or loss financial assets of Rs. 46 million.

The profits and losses recorded on a quarterly basis exhibit volatility due to accounting standards that require
changes in fair value, reflecting the fluctuation in share prices of investee companies, to be recognized in the
income statement.

Accordingly, the Group's net assets, valued at market price as of September 30, 2026, experienced a slight
decline from March 31, 2026, reaching Rs. 406.02 per share. However, the market price of Rs. 216.00 per share
indicates a substantial discount of 47% compared to the Net Asset Value (NAV).
