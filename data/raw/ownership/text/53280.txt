      CEO Review
      Dear Shareholders,

      Revenue increased by 9.5% YoY (Year-on-Year) to LKR 5.6 bn in the first half of FY27 (1HFY27), driven by
      higher oil palm output and selling prices. Profit after tax (PAT) increased marginally by 1.6% YoY to LKR
      1.75 bn, as higher operating costs and tax expense offset most of the revenue growth. Dairy remained
      loss-making for the half year, although its second-quarter loss narrowed significantly from the corre-
      sponding period last year.

      Group Performance
      Watawala Plantations PLC (CSE: WATA) generated revenue of LKR 5.6 bn for the six months ended 30
      September 2026, an increase of 9.5% YoY from the corresponding period last year. Oil palm revenue
      increased by 11.1% YoY, partly offset by a 2.9% YoY decline in dairy revenue.

      PAT was LKR 1.75 bn, an increase of 1.6% YoY from the corresponding period last year. Net assets per
      share were LKR 3.39 as at 30 September 2026, compared with LKR 3.92 a year earlier.

      During the period, the company acquired the remaining non-controlling interest in Lonach Dairy Lim-
      ited, making it a wholly owned subsidiary.


      Oil Palm
      The oil palm segment generated revenue of LKR 5.0 bn, an increase of 11.1% YoY from the corresponding
      period last year. Higher own-crop output and selling prices supported revenue growth. However, limit-
      ed bought-crop availability and lower oil extraction rates constrained overall production.

      Higher fertiliser, harvesting and milling costs reduced the gross margin to 61.5% in 1HFY27, from 62.6%
      in the corresponding period last year. Segment profit after tax increased by 1.8% YoY to LKR 1.85 bn,
      while the profit after tax margin declined to 37.0% from 40.4% in 1HFY26.

      Dairy
      Dairy revenue decreased by 2.9% YoY to LKR 561.1 mn, as lower milk sales volumes more than offset the
      benefit of higher average selling prices. Milk yield per cow improved during the second quarter, and
      new customer additions reduced concentration in the customer base.

      The business recorded a net loss of LKR 99.8 mn for the first half, compared with LKR 93.5 mn in the
      corresponding period last year. Higher effluent-management costs outweighed the benefit of lower
      feed costs and an improved gross margin. The second-quarter loss narrowed to LKR 35.3 mn from LKR
      68.0 mn in 2QFY26.


      Outlook
      We expect domestic palm oil demand to remain stable in the near term. Our immediate priorities
      are to improve extraction rates, maintain mill reliability, secure bought-crop supplies and control field
      costs. These actions are intended to support production and protect margins following the cost pres-
      sures experienced during the first half.

      In dairy, we will focus on improving milk yield, increasing the number of milking cows and reducing
      effluent-management costs. The reduction in the second-quarter loss is encouraging, but sustained
      improvement will depend on further productivity gains and tighter control of operating costs. Across
      both businesses, we will continue to improve operating efficiency while maintaining responsible agri-
      cultural practices.
