REVIEW OF OPERATIONS




   During the quarter under review, the Company operated against a backdrop of heightened global
   economic uncertainty, particularly arising from geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. In May 2026,
   the Central Bank of Sri Lanka increased its policy rate by 100 basis points in response to the resulting
   macroeconomic pressures. These developments contributed to increased market volatility and
   constrained trading opportunities, while adverse mark-to-market movements also impacted the
   Company’s trading portfolio.

   Against this challenging environment, First Capital Treasuries PLC recorded a Loss after Tax of
   Rs. 741 Mn for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 1.55 Bn in the
   corresponding quarter of the previous year. While market conditions presented significant challenges
   during the quarter, the Company remained focused on managing its portfolio prudently and positioning
   the business to respond to changing market conditions.

   The Company’s total investment in Government Securities increased to Rs. 60.35 Bn as at 30 June
   2026, from Rs. 53.60 Bn at the beginning of the financial year, reflecting the continued strength of the
   Company’s balance sheet and its commitment to its core business. The loss resulted in a drop in total
   net assets to Rs. 6.91 Bn as at 30 June 2026, compared to Rs. 7.65 Bn at the end of the previous quarter.

   While the quarter was undoubtedly challenging, the Company remains confident in the underlying
   strength of its business and its ability to navigate changing market conditions. The loss recorded during
   the quarter is viewed as a short-term impact arising largely from prevailing market volatility and
   adverse mark-to-market movements, rather than a reflection of the underlying strength of the business.
   We remain focused on disciplined risk management, prudent portfolio management and strengthening
   our position to capture opportunities as market conditions improve.

7   Financial Instruments- Fair Value Measurement
    The Company measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the
    measurements in accordance with SLFRS 13.

    Level - 1
    Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial instrument
    is regarded as quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those prices represent
    actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm's length basis.

    Level - 2
    Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or
    accessible for these securities, their fair value measures are determined using relevant information generated by market transactions
    involving comparable securities.

    Level - 3
    Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique includes
    inputs not based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument's valuation.
    This category includes instruments that are valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant unobservable
    adjustments or assumptions are required to reflect differences between the instruments.
