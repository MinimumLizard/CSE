Cargills Bank posts Profit after Tax Rs. 254 Million for the nine months ended 30 September 2026
Financial Highlights
           • Profit after tax stands at Rs. 254 Mn for the nine months ended 30 September 2026
           • Net loan book grows by 15% (Rs. 9.5 Bn) compared to December 2025 to reach Rs. 72.6 Bn (YoY growth
             of 20%)
           • Impaired loans (net of stage 3 impairment) to total loans (net basis) ratio at 5.79%
           • Total assets grow by 19% compared to December 2025 to reach Rs. 109.2 Bn (YoY growth of 23%)
           • Customer deposits grow by 24% to reach Rs. 82.1 Bn compared to December 2025 (YoY growth of 31%)
           • Total Capital Ratio at 17.60%
           • Capital base strengthened following the equity capital raise of Rs. 2.5 Bn through a Rights Issue earlier in
             the year
Cargills Bank’s results for the nine months ended 30 September 2026 reported a profit before tax of Rs. 513 Mn
reflecting a 17% drop when compared to the corresponding period in 2025. This was primarily driven by a significant
Rs. 380 Mn reduction in total other income alongside an 11% increase in total operating expenses and higher
impairment charges, which impact was partially offset by growth in both net interest income and net fee and
commission income.
Net interest income rose to Rs. 3,419 Mn, a 25% increase over the nine months of 2025. This growth was primarily
driven by loan growth reinforced with a strategic focus on repricing of deposits and advances to reflect the market
conditions to manage the NIM in an optimal manner, as reflected by the increase in NIM from 4.38% as at end 2025
to 4.65% in the period under review.
Net fee and commission income recorded an increase of Rs. 103 Mn compared to corresponding period in 2025. This
15% increase was supported by growth in fee income from deposits, loans and trade services along with decrease in
fee expenses related to credit and debit cards.
Further, total other income for the period decreased by 76% to Rs. 120 Mn. This was largely due to higher realized
capital gains on the derecognition of financial assets and net gains from financial assets at fair value through profit or
loss reported in the nine months of 2025.
Total operating expenses increased by 11% to Rs. 3,076 Mn. Personnel expenses rose by 17% due to salary
increments and adjustments aimed at reflecting market conditions and retaining talent. Depreciation and
amortization grew by 35%, primarily driven by investments in information technology and other infrastructure
upgrades. The Bank's Cost-to-Income Ratio stood at 71.13%, compared to 70.68% as at 31 December 2025.
The Bank significantly narrowed its Other Comprehensive Loss to Rs. 124 Mn, a 76% improvement from the Rs. 514
Mn loss in the nine months of 2025. This was supported by lower fair value losses on financial assets measured at fair
value through OCI. Consequently, Total Comprehensive Income turned positive at Rs. 130 Mn, reflecting a 165%
recovery in comparison to the corresponding period in 2025.
The Banking segment of the operating segments was the primary driver of performance, with profit before tax rising
to Rs. 539 Mn from Rs. 114 Mn in the nine months of 2025. This growth was fueled by a doubling in segment net
interest income, which reached Rs. 2,057 Mn. The Treasury and Investments segment reported a loss before tax of
Rs. 26 Mn, a decrease from the previous period’s high base of Rs. 506 Mn profit. This decrease was primarily due to
lower realized capital gains on the derecognition of financial assets and reduced net gains from financial assets at fair
value through profit or loss.
The Bank demonstrated prudence in asset quality through rigorous scrutiny and recovery actions. Total impairment
charge for the period was Rs. 310 Mn, representing an increase from the Rs. 162 Mn charge in the nine months of
2025.
The Bank’s gross asset quality showed an underlying improvement as the Impaired Loans (Stage 3) to Total Loans
(Gross basis) dropped to 10.78% from 12.00% as at end 2025. Impaired Loans (Net of Stage 3 Impairment) to Total
Loans (net basis) Ratio improved to 5.79% as of 30 September 2026 compared to 6.52% in December 2025 and 7.55%
as of 30 September 2025. The Stage 3 Provision Cover was maintained at 46.29% vs 45.65% as at end 2025.
Cargills Bank continues to maintain Capital Adequacy and Liquid Assets Ratios well within regulatory requirements.
The Total Capital Ratio improved to 17.60% from 17.12% as at end 2025. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) - Rupee
was at 125.21% while the LCR - All Currency was at 123.65%. The Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) stood at 120.85%
as at the reporting date.
Total assets of the Bank as of 30 September 2026 at Rs. 109.2 Bn reflected an increase of Rs. 17.5 Bn or 19% since
December 2025 (YoY growth of 23% since September 2025). The net loan book posted a steady growth of Rs. 9.5 Bn
or 15%, from Rs. 63.1 Bn to Rs. 72.6 Bn (YoY growth of 20% since September 2025).
Customer deposits grew by 24% to reach Rs. 82.1 Bn as at the reporting date (YoY growth of 31% since September
2025), reflecting deepening customer trust, successful deposit mobilization strategies and onboarding new
customers.
Earlier in the year, the Bank successfully raised equity capital of Rs. 2.5 Bn by way of a Rights issue of ordinary voting
shares with the objectives of meeting regulatory requirements, enhancing its capital base and supporting loan
growth. In concluding this exercise, the trust and confidence placed by the investors is appreciated and
acknowledged.

Assets and Liabilities Measured at Fair Value - Fair Value Hierarchy
Fair Value Hierarchy
The Bank measures the fair value using the following fair value hierarchy, which reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the
measurement.
• Level 1 : Fair value measurement using unadjusted quoted market prices
Inputs that are quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an active market for identical instruments.

• Level 2 : Fair value measurement using significant observable inputs
Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable either directly (i.e., as prices) or indirectly (i.e., derived from prices). This
category includes instruments valued using;
  (a) quoted prices in active markets for similar instruments,
  (b) quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered to be less active, or
  (c) other valuation techniques in which almost all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.

•Level 3 : Fair value measurement using significant unobservable inputs
Inputs that are unobservable.
This category includes all instruments for which the valuation technique includes inputs not based on observable data and the unobservable inputs
have a significant effect on the instrument’s valuation.
Fair value estimates obtained from models are adjusted for any other factors, such as liquidity risk or model uncertainties, to the extent that the
Bank believes that a third party market participant would take them into account in pricing a transaction.
