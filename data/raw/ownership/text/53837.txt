MANAGING DIRECTOR/CEO’S REVIEW



First Capital Holdings PLC, a subsidiary of JXG (Janashakthi Group), recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 1.27
billion for the six months ended 30 September 2026, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 3.43 billion in the
corresponding period of the previous financial year. For the second quarter of FY 2026/27, the Group recorded
a Loss after Tax of Rs. 540 million.

Capital markets are inherently cyclical, shaped by monetary policy, interest rate movements, market liquidity
and investor sentiment. Over four decades, First Capital has navigated multiple market cycles, adapting its
strategies and business operations through changing market conditions. This depth of experience continues to
inform our approach to managing risk, responding to market developments and pursuing opportunities across
the financial cycle. The first half of FY 2026/27 presented another challenging operating environment, requiring
disciplined execution and a prudent approach to business management.

Performance during the period was significantly influenced by a 100-basis-point increase in the monetary policy
rate in May 2026, driven by inflationary pressures linked to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Volatility
and downward pressure on government securities yields towards the latter part of the first quarter and during
the second quarter affected valuations and trading performance across the Group's fixed-income, debt and equity
portfolios.

Performance across business divisions
The impact of prevailing market conditions varied across the Group's business divisions, with fixed-income and
securities trading activities experiencing the greatest pressure.

The Primary Dealer division recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 537 million, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs.
1.57 billion in first half of FY 2025/26. This included a trading loss of Rs. 1.69 billion on government securities,
against a trading gain of Rs. 1.81 billion in the corresponding period last year. Net Interest Income moderated
to Rs. 687 million from Rs. 988 million.

The Corporate Dealing Securities division recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 834 million, compared to a Profit
after Tax of Rs. 1.78 billion in first half of FY 2025/26, reflecting challenging trading conditions across the debt
and equity markets.

The Corporate Finance Advisory division recorded a Profit after Tax of Rs. 69 million, compared to Rs. 25
million in the corresponding period last year, reflecting improved performance.

The Wealth Management division reported a Loss after Tax of Rs. 3 million, compared to a Profit after Tax of
Rs. 48 million in first half of FY 2025/26. Assets under Management stood at Rs. 92.4 billion as at 30 September
2026.

The Stock Brokering division remained profitable, recording a Profit after Tax of Rs. 12 million, compared to
Rs. 116 million in the corresponding period last year, reflecting lower trading volumes amid subdued equity
market activity.

Strategic priorities and outlook
Our priorities remain firmly centred on disciplined risk management, balance sheet strength and operational
efficiency. We continue to closely monitor market developments, exercise prudence in managing trading
positions and maintain a disciplined approach to capital and resource allocation, ensuring that business decisions
remain aligned with prevailing market conditions and our long-term strategic priorities.

We also focus on strengthening our market position, expanding customer reach and enhancing access to
investment opportunities through digital capabilities and improved service delivery. Deepening client
relationships, building on our institutional expertise and continually strengthening our governance, oversight
and risk management frameworks remain integral to our business priorities.

While market cycles will continue to shape near-term performance, First Capital's long-term ambitions remain
firmly on course. Backed by four decades of experience in Sri Lanka's capital markets, deep institutional
expertise and a disciplined approach to risk management, we are focused on navigating the current environment
with sound judgement while positioning the business to capitalise on opportunities as market conditions evolve.
Our commitment extends beyond managing the immediate market cycle to strengthening our competitive
position, advancing the development of Sri Lanka's capital markets and reinforcing the confidence of our clients
and stakeholders.

We remain firmly committed to strengthening First Capital’s leadership in Sri Lanka’s capital markets,
advancing our institutional capabilities and delivering sustainable, long-term value to our stakeholders.

8     Financial instruments- Fair Value Measurement
      The Company measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the
      measurements in accordance with SLFRS 13.

      Level - 1
      Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial instrument is
      regarded as quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those prices represent
      actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm's length basis.

      Level - 2
      Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or
      accessible for these securities, their fair value measures are determined using relevant information generated by market transactions
      involving comparable securities.

      Level - 3
      Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique includes
      inputs not based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument's valuation. This category
      includes instruments that are valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant unobservable adjustments or
      assumptions are required to reflect differences between the instruments.
