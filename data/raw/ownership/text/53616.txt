For the nine months ended 30th September 2026

       Nations Trust Bank sustains strong growth momentum
                            in Q3 2026

      Loan growth of LKR 155Bn, up 36% YoY
      Profit After Tax of LKR 20Bn, up 34% YoY
      Net Stage 3 Ratio of 0.72%
      Tier I Capital Ratio of 12.80%
      Total Capital Adequacy Ratio of 16.17%
      Return on Equity of 26.11%

Nations Trust Bank PLC (NTB) recorded a strong financial performance for the nine months
ended 30th September 2026, supported by sustained growth across its key business segments
and continued earnings momentum. The Bank recorded a Profit After Tax (PAT) of LKR 20Bn,
while maintaining healthy asset growth and a strong balance sheet during the period.

The Bank continued to build on its performance through a focus on growing its Consumer,
Commercial and Corporate Banking businesses, while building service excellence, advancing its
digital capabilities and exercising disciplined risk management. This approach enabled NTB to
sustain growth while maintaining the quality and resilience of its portfolio.

NTB’s performance was supported by healthy asset growth, stable Net Interest Margins (NIMs)
of 5.40%, and disciplined risk management, resulting in a Net Stage 3 Ratio of 0.72%. Return on
Equity (ROE) stood at 26.11%, reflecting the Bank’s continued earnings momentum and
effective deployment of capital.

Total income grew by 14% during the quarter, partly offset by costs associated with the
acquisition of HSBC Sri Lanka’s retail banking portfolio, and Day 1 provisions on the acquired
portfolio.

Commenting on the results, Nations Trust Bank Director and Chief Executive Officer, Hemantha
Gunetilleke said, “The strong performance that NTB has delivered during the first nine months
of 2026 reflects the continued momentum across our businesses and our focus on sustainable
growth. We have maintained a clear commitment to strengthening customer relationships and
service excellence. As we move into the final quarter of the year, we intend to build on this
progress and deliver long-term value for our customers and other stakeholders.”

Nations Trust Bank PLC serves customers across Consumer, Commercial and Corporate Banking
segments through an extensive network of physical and digital touchpoints. The Bank remains
focused on enabling customers through innovative banking solutions and advanced digital
capabilities. NTB is the issuer and sole acquirer of American Express Cards in Sri Lanka and
continues to hold a leading position in premium banking.

Level 1 - quoted market price (unadjusted): financial instruments with quoted prices in active markets.

instruments are valued using models where all significant inputs are observable.
Level 3 - valuation techniques with significant unobservable inputs: This category includes all instruments valued using valuation techniques where one or more significant inputs are unobservable.


There were no material changes in the fair value of financial assets categorised under Level 3 compared to the values reported as at 31 December 2025. The Group has not changed the valuation models and assumptions
used to measure the fair values of Level 3 financial instruments during the period ended 30 September 2026.

(5) Im p air m e nt o f F inancial A sse ts
    (a) Loans and Advances – ECL assessment
    A comprehensive Expected Credit Loss (ECL) assessment was undertaken, incorporating the impact of cash flow delays, prevailing macroeconomic conditions, geopolitical developments, and other relevant external risk factors. Based on the assessment, provisions
    have been maintained at levels deemed adequate to ensure appropriate coverage of the loan portfolio. Borrowers exhibiting elevated credit risk were appropriately classified under Stage 2 or Stage 3 in accordance with the Bank’s ECL framework. In addition,
    management overlays maintained for high-risk segments and sectors were reviewed and retained at appropriate levels to provide prudent buffers against potential adverse developments and uncertainty in the operating environment.



    (b) Other Financial Assets - Balances with Central Bank
    As at 30.09.2026, the Bank carried a receivable balance due from the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) relating to the Special Deposit Accounts (SDA) and the Incentive Scheme on Inward Workers’ Remittances introduced under previous regulatory directions. In
    December 2024, CBSL communicated the need to reassess the recoverability of these receivables. Based on management’s assessment of the settlement status and available information, an impairment provision has been recognised against the outstanding balance.
    Further, CBSL has formally confirmed in January 2026, that amounts claimed under the Rs. 2.00 per USD Incentive Scheme on Workers’ Remittances will not be settled. Accordingly, the Bank has written off the related receivable balance, and the impact has been
    recognised in the financial year 2025.

(9) SLFRS 3 – Business Combinations Disclosures

    On 1 May 2026, the Group completed the acquisition of the retail banking business of The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited, Colombo Branch (“HSBC”) in Sri Lanka (the “Acquired Portfolio”), comprising loans and advances to customers (including
    credit cards, term loans and overdrafts), customer deposits, the organised workforce and lease rights in respect of the branch network transferred.
    Control of the Acquired Portfolio transferred to the Group on 1 May 2026, which is the acquisition date for accounting purposes. As this date falls within the current reporting period, the transaction has been subjected to provisional accounting as a business
    combination under SLFRS 3 – Business Combinations and is reflected in the Statement of Financial Position as at 30 September 2026. Further to the provisional accounting reported in the interim financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026, the
    Group has identified the Credit Card Customer Relationship Intangible (CRI), Core Deposit Intangible (CDI) and Depositor Relationship Intangible (DRI) as the key, separately identifiable intangible assets arising from the acquisition. The measurement of their fair
    values and the related purchase price allocation are currently being finalised.
    Pending completion of this process, provisional goodwill continues to be recognised, reflecting the consideration transferred together with the provisional fair value of the net identifiable liabilities assumed in the business combination.
    In accordance with SLFRS 3, Business Combinations, the provisional amounts recognised, including goodwill, will be adjusted retrospectively during the measurement period, which shall not exceed one year from the acquisition date, upon finalisation of the fair
    values of the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed, including the identified intangible assets and the related deferred tax liabilities.
