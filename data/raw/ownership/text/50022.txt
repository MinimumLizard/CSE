  1. FINANCIAL REVIEW
     Sustains leadership as the largest NBFI with strong profitability and accelerated portfolio expansion, supported by robust asset growth and
     disciplined cost management
      ▪ Profit After Tax increases 16% YoY to Rs. 19.35 Bn
      ▪ Gross lending portfolio reaches Rs. 393 Bn as at 31 December 2025
      ▪ Net interest income grows 18% YoY to Rs. 36.54 Bn
      ▪ Total assets expand to Rs. 511.07 Bn, up 19% since March 2025
      ▪ Borrowings increase to Rs. 89.34 Bn to support rapid lending growth

     Strong Profitability and Income Growth
     LOLC Finance PLC, Sri Lanka’s largest non-bank financial institution (NBFI), delivered a resilient financial performance for the period ended 31
     December 2025, recording strong profitability alongside significant balance sheet expansion amid improving macroeconomic conditions.

     The Company reported a profit after tax (PAT) of Rs. 19.35 billion, representing a 16% year-on-year growth, driven primarily by higher net interest
     income, improved credit quality, and sustained business volumes across key lending segments.

     Gross income increased by 15% YoY to Rs. 60.75 billion, supported by strong lending momentum and improved fee-based income streams. Interest
     income rose by 12% to Rs. 57.02 billion, while interest expenses increased marginally by 2% to Rs. 20.48 billion, resulting in an 18% increase in net
     interest income to Rs. 36.54 billion.

     Net fee and commission income grew by 13% to Rs. 2.26 billion, reflecting higher transactional activity and service-related income. Although net
     gains / (losses) from trading declined sharply, this was offset by improved core income streams and the absence of one-off losses (derecognition loss
     on restructuring of SLISBs) recorded in the prior year, leading to total operating income increasing by 22% to Rs. 39.21 billion.

     Impairment and Cost Management
     Total impairment charges for the period amounted to Rs. 312 million, compared to a net impairment reversal of Rs. 5.12 billion in the previous year,
     which had benefited from significant reversals relating to Sri Lanka International Sovereign Bonds (SLISBs) restructuring. Excluding these
     exceptional items, the underlying credit performance of the loan portfolio remained stable, supported by improved recoveries and disciplined
     underwriting standards.

     Operating expenses increased moderately by 10% year-on-year to Rs. 18.27 billion. Despite this, LOLC Finance maintained strong cost discipline,
     with improved productivity and operating efficiency across its extensive branch network.

     Consequently, profit from operating activities remained stable at Rs. 20.62 billion, underscoring the Company’s ability to absorb cost increases while
     sustaining profitability.

     Financial Position and Lending Growth
     As at 31 December 2025, total assets stood at Rs. 511.07 billion, representing an increase of Rs. 81.34 billion (19%) compared to 31 March 2025.
     This growth was driven primarily by expansion in loans and advances, higher placements with banks, and increased investments in property and
     infrastructure.

     The gross lending portfolio reached Rs. 393 billion, with net loans and advances to customers increasing to Rs. 383.35 billion, up significantly from
     Rs. 297.51 billion as at March 2025, reaffirming the Company’s leadership in the NBFI sector and its strong market penetration across diversified
     lending products.

     Customer deposits grew to Rs. 241.65 billion, reflecting continued depositor confidence, while borrowings increased substantially to Rs. 89.34
     billion from Rs. 15.68 billion as at March 2025, providing funding support for rapid portfolio growth.

     Total shareholders’ equity stood at Rs. 147.62 billion, marginally lower than March 2025, mainly due to share repurchase made during the year,
     while maintaining a strong capital base.

     Capital & Liquidity
     LOLC Finance remains exceptionally well-capitalized with a strong equity base of over Rs. 147.62 billion, reflecting its prudent risk management
     practices and strong retained earnings base. As at 31st December 2025, the Company reported a Tier 1 Capital Ratio of 22.01%, significantly above
     the regulatory minimum of 10%, and a Total Capital Ratio of 21.78%, well in excess of the 17% minimum requirement. This substantial capital
     buffer enhances the Company’s resilience to economic volatility and underpins sustainable growth in its lending operations. With strong internal
     capital generation and effective management of risk-weighted assets, LOLC Finance is well-positioned to support future expansion while continuing
     to meet all prudential regulatory norms.

     With a network of 203 branches and continued focus on digital innovation, LOLC Finance remains agile in expanding access to credit, mobilizing
     deposits, and delivering enhanced value to stakeholders.

     The Company’s performance for the period ended 31 December 2025 underscores its continued leadership in Sri Lanka’s non-bank financial sector,
     characterized by solid profitability, strong balance sheet growth, and sound risk management, positioning it well for sustained performance in the
     remainder of the financial year.

  3. FAIR VALUE OF ASSETS & LIABILITIES
     The Company measures the fair value using the following fair value hierarchy, which reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the measurement. An
     analysis of fair value measurement of financial and non-financial assets and liabilities is provided below:

     Level 1
     Inputs that are quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an active market for identical instruments. When available, the Company measures the fair value of an
     instrument using active quoted prices or dealer price quotations, without any deduction for transaction costs. A market is regarded as active if transactions for
     asset or liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis.

     Level 2
     Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable either directly (i.e., as prices) or indirectly (i.e., derived from prices).
     This category includes instruments valued using;
     (a) quoted prices in active markets for similar instruments,
     (b) quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered to be less active, or
     (c) other valuation techniques in which almost all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.

     Level 3
     Inputs that are unobservable.
     This category includes financial instruments valued using techniques that rely on significant unobservable inputs. These may involve adjustments to quoted prices
     of similar instruments or models such as discounted cash flows. Key inputs and assumptions include interest rates, risk premiums, market prices, exchange rates,
     and price volatilities.

     Observable prices or model inputs such as market interest rates are usually available in the market for listed equity securities and Government Securities such as
     Treasury Bills and Treasury Bonds. Availability of observable prices and model inputs reduces the need for Management judgement and estimation while
     reducing uncertainty associated in determining the fair values.
