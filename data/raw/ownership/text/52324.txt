  1. FINANCIAL REVIEW
     Strong first quarter performance supported by robust lending growth and sustained balance sheet expansion
      ▪ Profit after tax increased by 4% to Rs. 5.40 billion.
      ▪ Gross lending portfolio expanded significantly reaching Rs. 437.75 Bn as at 30 June 2026
      ▪ Net interest income increased by 17% to Rs. 13.37 billion.
      ▪ Total assets expanded to Rs. 588.59 billion, an increase of Rs. 30.04 billion (5%) since 31 March 2026.
      ▪ Customer deposits and borrowings grew to Rs. 288.83 billion and Rs. 105.05 billion, respectively.

     Strong Profitability and Income Growth
     LOLC Finance PLC delivered a resilient financial performance for the three months ended 30 June 2026, reporting a profit after tax of Rs. 5.40 billion,
     an increase of 4% compared with the corresponding period of the previous year.

     Gross income increased by 27% to Rs. 23.99 billion, mainly driven by continued expansion of the lending portfolio. Interest income grew by 31% to
     Rs. 22.92 billion, reflecting higher lending volumes. Interest expenses increased by 56% to Rs. 9.55 billion, mainly due to higher funding costs arising
     from the growth in deposit liabilities and borrowings. Consequently, net interest income increased by 17% to Rs. 13.37 billion.

     Net fee and commission income remained stable at Rs. 734 million, while total operating income increased by 12% to Rs. 14.02 billion, supported by
     strong core business growth despite lower trading gains and other operating income compared with the corresponding period of the previous year.


     Asset Quality and Cost Management
     The Company recorded a net impairment charge of Rs. 235 million during the period, compared with Rs. 180 million in the corresponding period of
     the previous year, reflecting prudent provisioning in line with portfolio growth and prevailing macroeconomic conditions.

     Operating expenses increased by 19% to Rs. 6.94 billion, mainly attributable to personnel costs, depreciation, and continued investments in business
     expansion and operational infrastructure. Despite the increase in operating expenses, disciplined cost management enabled the Company to report a
     5% growth in profit before tax, which reached Rs. 6.84 billion.


     Financial Position and Lending Growth
     Total assets increased by Rs. 30.04 billion (5%) to Rs. 588.59 billion as at 30 June 2026, compared with Rs. 558.55 billion as at 31 March 2026. The
     growth was primarily driven by the expansion of the lending portfolio, increased placements with banks, and higher investments in financial assets
     measured at fair value through profit or loss.

     Gross loans and advances to customers increased by Rs. 14.7 billion (3.5%) to Rs. 437.75 billion during the quarter, reflecting continued business
     growth across the Company’s lending segments.

     Customer deposits increased by Rs. 17.33 billion (6%) to Rs. 288.83 billion, while borrowings increased by Rs. 6.64 billion (7%) to Rs. 105.05
     billion, providing funding to support continued asset growth. Total equity strengthened to Rs. 160.81 billion, supported by the profit earned during the
     period, resulting in the net asset value per share improving to Rs. 5.44 from Rs. 5.26 as at 31 March 2026.


     Capital & Liquidity
     LOLC Finance continues to maintain a strong capital position, underpinned by a robust equity base and prudent risk management practices. As at 30
     June 2026, total equity stood at Rs. 160.81 billion, while the Tier 1 and Total Capital Ratios were 22.43% and 22.21%, respectively, remaining well
     above the regulatory minimum requirements of 10% and 17%. This strong capital position provides a solid foundation to support future business
     growth and withstand potential economic uncertainties.

     The Company also maintained a healthy liquidity position, supported by higher placements with banks, a well-diversified funding base, and adequate
     liquid assets to meet operational and growth requirements.

     With a network of 205 branches, ongoing investments in digital transformation, and continued enhancements to its operational infrastructure, LOLC
     Finance remains well positioned to strengthen its market leadership, promote financial inclusion, and deliver sustainable value to its stakeholders.

     The Company has commenced the new financial year on a strong footing, delivering resilient earnings growth alongside continued expansion of its
     balance sheet. Backed by its market leadership, diversified lending portfolio, strong capital and liquidity positions, and disciplined risk management
     framework, LOLC Finance is well positioned to sustain its growth momentum and create long-term value for all stakeholders.

  3. FAIR VALUE OF ASSETS & LIABILITIES
     The Company measures the fair value using the following fair value hierarchy, which reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the measurement. An
     analysis of fair value measurement of financial and non-financial assets and liabilities is provided below:

     Level 1
     Inputs that are quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an active market for identical instruments. When available, the Company measures the fair value of an
     instrument using active quoted prices or dealer price quotations, without any deduction for transaction costs. A market is regarded as active if transactions for
     asset or liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis.

     Level 2
     Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable either directly (i.e., as prices) or indirectly (i.e., derived from prices).
     This category includes instruments valued using;
     (a) quoted prices in active markets for similar instruments,
     (b) quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered to be less active, or
     (c) other valuation techniques in which almost all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.

     Level 3
     Inputs that are unobservable.
     This category includes financial instruments valued using techniques that rely on significant unobservable inputs. These may involve adjustments to quoted prices
     of similar instruments or models such as discounted cash flows. Key inputs and assumptions include interest rates, risk premiums, market prices, exchange rates,
     and price volatilities.

     Observable prices or model inputs such as market interest rates are usually available in the market for listed equity securities and Government Securities such as
     Treasury Bills and Treasury Bonds. Availability of observable prices and model inputs reduces the need for Management judgement and estimation while
     reducing uncertainty associated in determining the fair values.
