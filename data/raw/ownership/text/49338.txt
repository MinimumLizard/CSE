FINANCIAL REVIEW



Cable Solutions PLC recorded good growth in the first six months of the financial year ending
March 2026 recording a 19% increase in Group attributable net profit to LKR 257mn driven by a
10% YoY growth in revenues, good cost control and lower net finance costs.

For Q2 FY26, Group revenue stood at LKR 873mn, representing a 5% increase YoY compared to
LKR 832mn in Q2 FY25. The topline growth was broad-based, supported by improved volumes
from both existing and newly onboarded clients. The company is seeing good demand from
export and deemed export customers.

Gross profit increased to LKR 281mn, up 16% YoY as gross margin increased to 32.2% from 29.2%
in Q2 FY25, primarily due to a favorable sales mix in comparison to the previous year and despite
a gradual uptick in copper prices.

Group operating profit (EBIT) rose to LKR 152mn, registering a 17% YoY growth, reflecting
improved cost discipline and better operating leverage.

Group net profit for the quarter was LKR 122mn, up 25% YoY, translating to a net profit margin
of 14.1%. This improvement reflects strong operational performance and a decline in net interest
costs. The Indian operation made a loss of about LKR 4mn for the quarter compared to a loss of
LKR 5.5mn in the year ago period and LKR7.3mn in the preceding quarter. Contributions from
India are expected to turn profitable in subsequent quarters as client traction is improving.

Compared to the end of FY25, inventory levels have reduced while there has been an increase in
trade receivables. The latter in line with the growing scale of operations, although the Company
continues to emphasize working capital discipline.

The Group’s net debt-to-equity ratio has fallen further over the six-month period to just 5% as at
end September 2025 from 11% at the end of FY25. Internal cash generation has continued to
improve and capex commitments for the ERP and machinery upgrades are being managed within
the Company's liquidity profile.

Going forward, the Company expects to maintain revenue momentum through increased
contribution from new product lines (e.g., EV cables, FLRY variants), deeper penetration into the
U.S. market (via the newly formed subsidiary) and strategic investments in R&D, certifications,
and digital tools. It also seems the US tariff related noises are starting to ease. However, the recent
volatility in commodity prices, including Copper, needs to be watched carefully and management
will continue to proactively manage customer pricing and margins.
