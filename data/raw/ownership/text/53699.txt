2. The interim financial statements of the company for the 9 months ended 30 September 2026 have been prepared in accordance with Sri Lanka

The same accounting policies and methods of computation as stated in the audited financial statements 2025 have been followed in preparation of

In accordance with LKAS 34.19, these interim financial statements do not comply fully with Sri Lanka Accounting Standards (SLFRSs) as the
Company has elected to apply the Statement of Alternative Tratment (SoAT) issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri

Accordingly, these interim financial statements are prepared on the alternative basis as described below.


2.1 Application of Statement of Alternative Treatment (SoAT) – Q3 Interim Financial Reporting

SLFRS 17 – Insurance Contracts is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026. However, in accordance with the
Statement of Alternative Treatment (SoAT) and the subsequent letter dated 28 October 2026 issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri
Lanka (CA Sri Lanka), insurers have been granted the option to prepare interim financial statements for the first three quarters of 2026 by applying
SLFRS 4 – Insurance Contracts, in response to the implementation challenges faced by the insurance industry provided that the expected impact of
SLFRS 17 and SLFRS 9 is adequately disclosed.

The Company has elected to apply this relief for the interim financial statements for the nine months ended 30 September 2026.

Accordingly, for this interim period:

• Insurance contracts continue to be recognised, measured, and presented in accordance with SLFRS 4 - Insurance Contracts.
• SLFRS 9 - Financial Instruments has not been applied, consistent with the SoAT requirement that SLFRS 9 shall not be applied during interim
periods where SLFRS 4 is continued.

CA Sri Lanka has not yet decided the reporting basis for the fourth quarter or for the annual financial statements for the year ending 31st December
2026.

2.1.1. Progress of SLFRS 17 and SLFRS 9 Implementation

The Company has made substantive progress during the three quarters of the financial year towards full implementation of SLFRS 17 and SLFRS 9,
including:

• Completion of major actuarial model builds for insurance contract measurement
• Refinement of discount rate methodologies and risk adjustment frameworks
• Strengthening of governance, controls, and data validation processes

The Company is currently in the process of validating its SLFRS 17 results and determining the fair values of transactions relating to the opening
balance.

2.1.2. Expected Impact of SLFRS 17 and SLFRS 9

The adoption of these standards are expected to have a material impact on the Company's financial position and financial performance.
The expected impact by area is summarised below.


Area                                 Expected Impact
Financial Assets – SLFRS 9           Classification and measurement: Financial assets currently classified under LKAS 39 as held-to-maturity, loans
                                     and receivables, available-for-sale and fair value through profit or loss will be reclassified as measured at
                                     amortised cost, fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI) or fair value through profit or loss
                                     (FVTPL). The classification depends on (i) the business model for managing each portfolio, assessed together
                                     with the asset-liability management of the related takaful liabilities, and (ii) whether contractual cash flows are
                                     solely payments of principal and profit (SPPI). Shari'ah-compliant instruments (e.g. Sukuk, Mudarabah and
                                     Wakalah investments) will be assessed individually for SPPI; instruments whose returns depend on the
                                     performance of underlying assets are likely to be measured at FVTPL. Equity investments will be measured at
                                     FVTPL unless the irrevocable FVOCI election is made, in which case fair value gains and losses will not be
                                     reclassified to profit or loss on disposal.

                                     Impairment: The LKAS 39 incurred-loss model will be replaced by a forward-looking expected credit loss (ECL)
                                     model using a three-stage approach (12-month ECL for Stage 1; lifetime ECL for Stages 2 and 3, based on
                                     significant increase in credit risk) and probability-weighted macroeconomic scenarios. This is expected to bring
                                     forward the recognition of impairment, particularly on placements with licensed finance companies and on
                                     corporate debt instruments.

                                     Interaction with SLFRS 17: Takaful and retakaful receivables, which are currently presented as financial assets
                                     and assessed for impairment under LKAS 39, will form part of takaful and retakaful contract balances under
                                     SLFRS 17 and will therefore fall outside SLFRS 9.

                                     Transition: SLFRS 9 will be applied retrospectively. The cumulative effect will be recognised in opening retained
                                     earnings and other components of equity at that date, without restating comparatives except where the
                                     classification overlay in SLFRS 17 is applied.


Insurance Contract Liabilities       Currently, under SLFRS 4, family takaful liabilities are valued by the Appointed Actuary in line with the IRCSL
                                     valuation basis and are subject to a liability adequacy test. Under SLFRS 17, contracts will be aggregated into
                                     portfolios and divided into annual cohorts, which are grouped by profitability at initial recognition (onerous, no
                                     significant possibility of becoming onerous, and remaining). Each group will be measured as the total of:
                                     (a) fulfilment cash flows, being the probability-weighted present value of future cash flows within the contract
                                     boundary, discounted at current rates that reflect the characteristics and liquidity of the liabilities;
                                     (b) an explicit risk adjustment for non-financial risk; and
                                     (c) a Contractual Service Margin (CSM) representing unearned profit, which will be released to profit or loss as
                                     takaful contract services are provided, based on coverage units.

                                     The General Measurement Model is expected to apply to long-term family takaful contracts. The Variable Fee
                                     Approach will apply to contracts with direct participation features (e.g. investment-linked plans where
                                     participants share in the returns of a clearly identified pool of underlying items), where they meet the eligibility
                                     criteria. The Premium Allocation Approach (PAA) is expected to apply to group family takaful contracts with a
                                     coverage period of one year or less. Investment components (e.g. maturity and surrender benefits) will be
                                     excluded from takaful revenue and takaful service expenses.

Equity                               SLFRS 17 transition: SLFRS 17 will be applied retrospectively from the transition date. The Fair Value Approach
                                     is expected to be used; this is expected for all cohorts of long-term family takaful contracts.

                                     On transition, the following will be recognised in opening retained earnings, net of deferred tax: (i) the
                                     derecognition of SLFRS 4 balances (including takaful contract liabilities, unearned contribution reserves,
                                     deferred acquisition costs, and takaful and retakaful receivables and payables); (ii) the recognition of SLFRS 17
                                     takaful and retakaful contract assets and liabilities, including the CSM and risk adjustment at transition.

                                     SLFRS 9 transition: The remeasurement arising from reclassification and ECL at the transaction date will be
                                     recognised in opening retained earnings. The available-for-sale reserve will be reclassified to the FVOCI
                                     reserve or to retained earnings, as appropriate.

Takaful Revenue and Profit or Loss   Takaful revenue will no longer correspond to gross written contributions. Instead, it will reflect the services
                                     provided in the period: the release of expected claims and expenses, the change in the risk adjustment, the
                                     release of CSM and the allocation of acquisition cash flows. Investment components will be excluded. Profit or
                                     loss will be presented as a takaful service result, separately from net takaful finance income or expense and
                                     investment income.

                                     Only directly attributable expenses will be included in takaful service expenses; other expenses will be
                                     presented as other operating expenses. Losses on onerous groups will be recognised immediately. Changes in
                                     estimates relating to future service will adjust the CSM rather than profit or loss, which reduces volatility from

At inception of a contract, the company assesses whether a contract is, or contains, a lease. A contract is, or contains, a lease if the contract conveys
the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. To assess whether a contract conveys the right to
control the use of an identified asset, the company assesses whether:

– the contract involves the use of an identified asset

– the company has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the asset throughout the period of use; and



– the company has the right to direct the use of the asset

The company recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability at the lease commencement date. The right-of-use assets of the company consist of
