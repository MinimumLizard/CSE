  1. FINANCIAL REVIEW
     Sustains leadership as the largest NBFI with strong profitability and portfolio expansion, backed by solid asset growth and efficient cost
     management
      ▪ Profit After Tax surges 72% YoY to Rs. 14.02 Bn.
      ▪ Loan book expands by Rs. 54.42 Bn. in six months to Rs. 351.93 Bn
      ▪ Net interest income up 16% YoY to Rs. 23.94 Bn.
      ▪ Total assets increase to Rs. 466.26 Bn., growing 8.5% since March 2025
      ▪ Borrowings rise to Rs. 50.16 Bn. to fuel lending portfolio growth

     Strong Profitability and Income Growth
     LOLC Finance PLC, Sri Lanka’s largest non-bank financial institution (NBFI), continued its strong performance in the first half of FY2025/26,
     delivering robust profitability and steady balance sheet expansion for the quarter ended 30 September 2025.

     The Company reported a profit after tax (PAT) of Rs. 14.02 billion, reflecting a remarkable 72% year-on-year growth, driven by higher net interest
     margins, improved asset quality, and enhanced operational efficiency.

     Gross income for the period increased by 8% year-on-year to Rs. 39.51 billion, supported by both lending growth and higher fee-based income.
     Interest income grew by 8% to Rs. 36.83 billion, while interest expenses declined by 5% to Rs. 12.89 billion, resulting in a 16% increase in net
     interest income to Rs. 23.94 billion.

     Net fee and commission income rose by 15% to Rs. 1.50 billion, reflecting continued expansion in service-related activities and higher transaction
     volumes. Although net gain from trading declined by 44%, other operating income remained resilient, supported by improved collections and
     recoveries. Consequently, total operating income reached Rs. 25.91 billion, up 15% compared to the previous year.

     Impairment and Cost Management
     The Company recorded a net impairment reversal of Rs. 3.2 million, compared to a charge of Rs. 1.15 billion in the corresponding period last year,
     underscoring a significant improvement in credit quality and recoveries across portfolios.

     Operating expenses increased moderately by 10% year-on-year to Rs. 12.02 billion. Despite this, LOLC Finance maintained strong cost discipline,
     with improved productivity and operating efficiency across its extensive branch network.

     As a result, profit from operating activities rose by 34% to Rs. 13.90 billion, reflecting robust operational performance.

     Financial Position and Lending Growth
     As at 30 September 2025, total assets stood at Rs. 466.26 billion, an increase of Rs. 36.53 billion (8.5%) since 31 March 2025, driven by loan book
     expansion and higher placements with banks.

     The net loan portfolio grew by Rs. 54.42 billion during the six months to reach Rs. 351.93 billion, reaffirming LOLC Finance’s leadership in credit
     intermediation and its ability to capture market demand across key lending segments.

     Customer deposits rose to Rs. 238.58 billion, reflecting continued customer confidence, while borrowings increased sharply to Rs. 50.16 billion from
     Rs. 15.68 billion, supporting portfolio growth and liquidity needs.

     On the equity side, total shareholders’ funds stood at Rs. 142.43 billion, compared to Rs. 149.52 billion at the beginning of the year, primarily
     reflecting the impact of share repurchase made during the period.

     Capital & Liquidity
     LOLC Finance remains exceptionally well-capitalized with a strong equity base of over Rs. 142.43 billion, reflecting its prudent risk management
     practices and strong retained earnings base. As at 30th September 2025, the Company reported a Tier 1 Capital Ratio of 19.08%, significantly above
     the regulatory minimum of 10%, and a Total Capital Ratio of 18.85%, well in excess of the 17% minimum requirement. This substantial capital
     buffer enhances the Company’s resilience to economic volatility and underpins sustainable growth in its lending operations. With strong internal
     capital generation and effective management of risk-weighted assets, LOLC Finance is well-positioned to support future expansion while continuing
     to meet all prudential regulatory norms.

     With a network of 204 branches and continued focus on digital innovation, LOLC Finance remains agile in expanding access to credit, mobilizing
     deposits, and delivering enhanced value to stakeholders.

     LOLC Finance’s strong first-half performance underscores its leadership in the non-bank financial sector, marked by robust profitability, solid
     balance sheet growth, and sound asset quality. With strategic lending momentum and prudent risk management, the Company is well-positioned to
     deliver strong results for the remainder of the financial year.

  3. FAIR VALUE OF ASSETS & LIABILITIES
     The Company measures the fair value using the following fair value hierarchy, which reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the measurement. An
     analysis of fair value measurement of financial and non-financial assets and liabilities is provided below:

     Level 1
     Inputs that are quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an active market for identical instruments. When available, the Company measures the fair value of an
     instrument using active quoted prices or dealer price quotations, without any deduction for transaction costs. A market is regarded as active if transactions for
     asset or liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis.

     Level 2
     Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable either directly (i.e., as prices) or indirectly (i.e., derived from prices).
     This category includes instruments valued using;
     (a) quoted prices in active markets for similar instruments,
     (b) quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered to be less active, or
     (c) other valuation techniques in which almost all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.

     Level 3
     Inputs that are unobservable.
     This category includes financial instruments valued using techniques that rely on significant unobservable inputs. These may involve adjustments to quoted prices
     of similar instruments or models such as discounted cash flows. Key inputs and assumptions include interest rates, risk premiums, market prices, exchange rates,
     and price volatilities.

     Observable prices or model inputs such as market interest rates are usually available in the market for listed equity securities and Government Securities such as
     Treasury Bills and Treasury Bonds. Availability of observable prices and model inputs reduces the need for Management judgement and estimation while
     reducing uncertainty associated in determining the fair values.
