Financial Performance Summary
Pan Asia Banking Corporation PLC delivered a strong financial performance for the nine months ended
30 September 2026, recording a Profit After Tax of Rs. 3.59 billion, reflecting the strength of its core
banking franchise, disciplined execution of its growth strategy, and continued focus on sustainable value
creation. The Bank achieved healthy growth across its key business segments while maintaining a
prudent approach to risk management and capital preservation.

The Bank's performance was underpinned by broad-based growth in both fund-based and fee-based
income streams, supported by expanding customer relationships, increased business volumes, and a
diversified revenue base. Continued investments in business development, digital capabilities, and
operational enhancements strengthened the Bank's ability to capitalize on growth opportunities while
delivering an enhanced customer experience.

The period also witnessed significant progress in strengthening the Bank's balance sheet. Strong growth
in assets, loans, and deposits was complemented by improving asset quality, low impairment levels, and
capital and liquidity ratios maintained comfortably above regulatory requirements. These achievements
reinforce Pan Asia Bank's financial resilience and position the Bank strongly to pursue sustainable growth
while delivering long-term value to shareholders and other stakeholders.

Income Performance
For the nine months ended 30 September 2026, Pan Asia Bank reported a commendable 26% year-on-
year growth in interest income to Rs. 28.62 billion, driven by continued expansion of its lending portfolio
and improved asset yields. This performance reflects the Bank’s strategic emphasis on strengthening its
Corporate and Business Banking franchises during the latter part of the previous year, creating a solid
platform for sustainable credit growth and enhanced customer engagement.

Interest expense increased by 37% to Rs. 17.94 billion, primarily due to the expansion of the deposit base
and borrowings during the period. Consequently, Net Interest Income (NII) increased by 12% to Rs. 10.68
billion. The Bank's Net Interest Margin (NIM) moderated to 4.16% from 4.55% in the corresponding period
of 2025, reflecting the combined impact of balance sheet growth, funding cost dynamics and the timing
differences in the repricing of assets and liabilities.

Demonstrating the strength of its diversified revenue base, net fee and commission income grew by 17%
to Rs. 2.18 billion. The growth was supported by the expansion of the Bank’s lending portfolio, higher
transaction volumes across key business segments, increased customer card utilization and continued
momentum in trade-related business activities. Trade finance, card-related services and lending-related
fees remained key contributors to the Bank’s non-funded income performance.

Meanwhile, net gains from trading and gains from the derecognition of financial assets measured at
FVOCI recorded declines of 13% and 74% respectively compared to the corresponding period of the
previous year. This was mainly attributable to the upward movement in Treasury bill and Treasury bond
yields during the period, which reduced market valuations of fixed-income securities and consequently
lowered mark-to-market and realization gains.

Despite these market-driven challenges, Pan Asia Bank continued to demonstrate the resilience of its
business model and earnings capacity, delivering a 7% increase in total operating income to Rs. 12.93
billion. The Bank’s performance reflects the effectiveness of its balanced growth strategy, supported by
a healthy mix of fund-based and fee-based income streams, while remaining well-positioned to navigate
an evolving operating environment and capitalize on future growth opportunities.

Asset Quality and Impairment Expenses
Pan Asia Bank maintained a prudent and forward-looking approach to credit risk management during the
nine months ended 30 September 2026, while continuing to strengthen the quality of its loan portfolio.
The Bank benefited from improving macroeconomic conditions, favourable movements in forward-
looking economic factors incorporated into its Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework, and the positive
impact of strengthened credit underwriting and portfolio monitoring practices implemented over recent
years.

As a result, impairment charges remained low during the period despite continued growth in the lending
portfolio. The Bank's disciplined credit risk management framework, proactive monitoring of borrower
performance, and focused recovery initiatives contributed to an improvement in portfolio quality and a
reduction in non-performing exposures.

The impairment outcome for the period was further supported by enhancements to the Bank's credit risk
assessment methodologies and refinements to key ECL model parameters, including Probability of
Default (PD) and Loss Given Default (LGD) assumptions, implemented following independent model
validation reviews. These enhancements strengthened the accuracy and risk sensitivity of the Bank's
impairment assessment process while ensuring prudent provisioning levels.

Reflecting these improvements, the Gross Stage 3 Loan Ratio improved to 3.67% from 4.62%, while the
Net Stage 3 Loan Ratio improved to 1.44% from 1.73% at end-2025. The reduction in Stage 3 exposures,
together with improvements in risk assessment methodologies and a more favourable operating
environment, contributed to a lower impairment burden during the period.

The Stage 3 Provision Coverage Ratio stood at 60.87% as at 30 September 2026. The Bank continues to
maintain prudent provisioning buffers and a conservative approach to risk management, supporting
balance sheet resilience and positioning the Bank to navigate evolving economic conditions while
pursuing sustainable growth.
Operational Efficiency
During the nine months ended 30 September 2026, Pan Asia Bank continued to invest strategically in
strengthening its technology capabilities, digital infrastructure, and business platforms to support long-
term growth and elevate customer experience. As a result, operating expenses increased by 14%
compared to the corresponding period of the previous year.

Consequently, the Bank’s Cost-to-Income Ratio stood at 51.49%, compared to 48.94% recorded during
the preceding period. The increase reflects the Bank’s deliberate investments in future-ready capabilities
aimed at enhancing operational agility, digital service delivery, and business scalability.

Despite these investments, Pan Asia Bank remained focused on driving operational excellence through
process improvements, automation initiatives, and the wider adoption of digital channels. These
measures contributed to greater operational efficiency, improved productivity, and enhanced business
resilience, enabling the Bank to effectively support growing customer volumes while creating sustainable
long-term value for all stakeholders.

Taxation
Taxes and levies on financial services increased by 11% during the period, reflecting the Bank's improved
operating profitability and continued business growth. Despite the increase in operating profit, income
tax expense declined compared to the corresponding period of the previous year, primarily due to the
reversal of excess income tax provisions relating to prior years.

The Bank remains committed to maintaining robust tax governance and compliance standards,
supported by prudent tax planning and proactive engagement with regulatory authorities. This disciplined
approach continues to reinforce a transparent, sustainable, and well-governed tax position while
supporting long-term value creation for stakeholders.

Profitability and Returns
Pan Asia Bank delivered a strong financial performance during the nine months ended 30 September
2026, with Profit After Tax increasing by 19% year-on-year, underscoring the strength of the Bank’s core
business operations and earnings resilience. The growth was supported by healthy expansion in both
fund-based and non-fund-based income streams, reflecting the Bank’s ability to capitalize on business
opportunities across its key operating segments.

The Bank maintained a healthy Net Interest Margin through prudent balance sheet management,
optimized asset-liability positioning, and a stable funding base. This enabled the Bank to sustain strong
earnings momentum despite prevailing market challenges and evolving economic conditions.

Key profitability indicators remained robust, with Return on Equity (ROE) and Return on Assets (ROA)
reflecting the strength of the Bank’s core income generation capabilities, improved asset quality, and
disciplined execution of its strategic priorities. Continued focus on operational efficiency, cost discipline,
and productivity enhancements further contributed to the Bank’s overall financial performance.
Supported by a resilient business model, a diversified revenue base, and a strong capital and funding
position, Pan Asia Bank remains well-positioned to sustain its growth trajectory and deliver long-term
value to all stakeholders while navigating the evolving macroeconomic landscape.

Other Comprehensive Income
Fair value losses were recorded on government securities classified under FVOCI, mainly Rupee Treasury
Bills and Treasury Bonds, due to increases in market interest rates during the period. These are mark-to-
market adjustments arising from yield movements and do not impact the Bank’s underlying earnings
performance.

Balance Sheet Growth
Pan Asia Bank continued its strong growth trajectory during the nine months ended 30 September 2026,
with total assets increasing by 22% during the 9 months ended 30th September 2026 to Rs. 374.45 billion,
reflecting sustained business momentum and the successful execution of the Bank’s growth strategy.

The Bank’s gross loans and advances portfolio expanded by 16% to Rs. 251.94 billion, supported by
healthy credit demand across the Corporate, SME, and Retail Banking segments. This growth was driven
by the Bank’s customer-centric approach, targeted sectoral focus, and prudent credit underwriting
practices, enabling it to deepen customer relationships while maintaining sound asset quality.

Customer deposits recorded a robust growth of 19% year-on-year to Rs. 274.89 billion as at 30 September
2026, reaffirming the confidence and trust placed in the Bank by its customers. The growth was broad-
based across the deposit portfolio, with strong contributions from both term deposits and low-cost CASA
balances, further strengthening the Bank’s funding franchise and liquidity profile.

The expansion of the balance sheet was complemented by the maintenance of strong liquidity buffers,
supported by healthy cash reserves and continued growth in both local and foreign currency deposit
bases. These factors reinforced the Bank’s funding stability and provided a solid foundation to support
future business expansion.

The consistent growth in assets, advances, and deposits highlights the strength of Pan Asia Bank’s
business model, the resilience of its customer franchise, and its ability to deliver sustainable growth
while maintaining a strong balance sheet and prudent risk management framework. The Bank remains
well-positioned to capitalize on emerging opportunities and create long-term value for its stakeholders.

Capital and Liquidity
The Bank maintained a strong capital and liquidity position during the period, with all regulatory ratios
remaining comfortably above minimum requirements. Capital adequacy remained robust, with a
Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio and Tier 1 Ratio of 14.34%, and a Total Capital Ratio of 15.67%,
providing a solid buffer to support growth while maintaining prudent risk coverage.

Liquidity indicators continued to demonstrate resilience, reflecting effective balance sheet management
and a stable funding profile. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) stood well above regulatory thresholds,
with the Rupee LCR at 138.92% and the All Currency LCR at 138.98%, while the Net Stable Funding Ratio
(NSFR) remained strong at 119.14%.

The Bank’s Leverage Ratio of 7.54% further underscores its balance sheet strength, reinforcing overall
financial resilience and providing capacity to sustain future expansion in a disciplined manner.

Outlook and Strategic Direction
Pan Asia Bank enters the final quarter of 2026 with strong business momentum, supported by healthy
balance sheet growth, improving asset quality, and robust capital and liquidity positions. The Bank
remains focused on accelerating quality growth across its Corporate, SME, and Retail Banking segments
while maintaining disciplined risk management practices and prudent capital stewardship.

The Bank will continue to invest in digital innovation, process automation, data-driven decision-making,
and customer experience enhancement initiatives to strengthen operational efficiency, deepen
customer relationships, and support long-term business scalability. Supported by a diversified business
model, a stable funding base, and resilient financial fundamentals, Pan Asia Bank is well positioned to
navigate evolving market conditions, capitalize on emerging opportunities, and deliver sustainable long-

As the Bank approaches the milestone of Rs. 375 billion in total assets, management remains committed
to creating lasting value through disciplined execution, customer-centric innovation, and sustainable
growth, while further strengthening Pan Asia Bank's position as one of Sri Lanka's most trusted and
progressive financial institutions.

5.    Fair Value of Financial Instruments

5.1   Financial Instruments Recorded at Fair Value

      The following is a description of how fair values are determined for financial instruments that are recorded at fair value using valuation techniques. These incorporate the Bank’s
      estimate of assumptions that a market participant would make when valuing the instruments.

      (a) Derivatives

      Derivative products valued with market-observable inputs are mainly currency swaps and forward foreign exchange contracts. The most frequently applied valuation techniques
      include forward exchange spot and forward premiums.

      (b) Financial Assets at Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income

      FVOCI financial assets primarily consist of unquoted equity securities and government securities. Government securities are valued using the published yield curves while unquoted
      equity securities are valued using prices of similar assets in quoted market prices in the active markets as at the reporting date.

      (c) Financial Assets at Fair Value through Profit or Loss

      FVPL financial assets measured at fair value are the government securities and units trust securities. Sri Lanka government securities are valued using yield curves published by the
      Central Bank of Sri Lanka. The Bank uses net asset values of units as at the reporting date as the market value of units.

      (d) Property, Plant & Equipment

      Freehold land and buildings are carried at revalued amount, being their fair value at the revaluation date less any subsequent accumulated depreciation and subsequent accumulated
      impairment losses.

5.2 Determination of Fair Value and Fair Value Hierarchy

      The Bank uses the following hierarchy for determining and disclosing the fair value of financial instruments by valuation technique.

      Level 1: Quoted (unadjusted) prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.

      Level 2: Other techniques for which all inputs which have a significant effect on the recorded fair value are observable, either directly or indirectly.

      Level 3: Techniques which use inputs that have a significant effect on the recorded fair value that are not based on observable market data.

      Also, the Bank has not changed the valuation models and assumptions used to measure the fair values of Level 3 financial instruments/assets during the 9 months period 2026.

Fair Value of Financial Assets and Liabilities Not Carried at Fair Value
The following describes the methodologies and assumptions used to determine fair values for those financial instruments which are not already recorded at fair value in the Financial Statements.

Assets for which Fair Value Approximates Carrying Value
For financial assets and financial liabilities that have a short term maturity, it is assumed that the carrying value approximates their fair value. This assumption is applied for Cash and Cash Equivalents, Balances with Central Bank of Sri Lanka, Placements with Banks
and Reverse Repurchase Agreements. This assumption is also applied to Call Money Borrowings, Balances with Foreign Banks, Repurchase Agreements, Demand Deposits, Margin Deposits, Savings Accounts without a specific maturity, floating rate instruments
and fixed rate instrumemts having maturities within 12 months.

Fixed Rate Financial Instruments
The fair value of fixed rate financial assets and liabilities (other than assets and liabilities with maturities within 12 months) carried at amortised cost are estimated by comparing market interest rates when they were first recognised with current market rates for similar
financial instruments.

       The on-going armed conflict in the Middle East has contributed to heighten the geopolitical and economic uncertainty globally, including
       volatility in energy prices, disruptions to trade and logistics and tightening global financial conditions, with indirect implications for the Sri
       Lankan economy. While the Bank did not experience any significant direct financial impact arising from the conflict, certain indirect
       effects were observed among customer segments exposed to higher import costs, energy price fluctuations and supply chain disruptions.

       The Bank closely monitored these developments and considered their potential impact on credit risk, liquidity and overall financial
       performance of the Bank. The conditions arising from the conflict existed at the reporting date and, where applicable, the related effects
       have been recognised in these Financial Statements.
