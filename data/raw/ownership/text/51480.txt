Group Chief Executive Officer’s Review – Interim Financial Statements

Dear stakeholder,

I am pleased to present the results of Capital Alliance Holdings Plc (CAL) for the year ending 31st March
2026.

The group recorded a profit of LKR 104 mn for the quarter, and a cumulative net profit of LKR 2.7bn for
the year. The weaker than expected performance for the quarter is an outcome of the geopolitical situation,
where a strong performance across all business units in FY26 was partially undone by the unfolding war in
Iran. Significant trading profits in January and February 2026 were reversed in March following the onset
of the war.

At the time of writing this letter, the war in the Middle East appears to be entering a de-escalation phase.
But given the twists and turns in a complex mediation process, with two sides that appear far apart on many
issues, the likelihood of a lasting resolution within a short timeframe remains low. The macroeconomic
fallout to Sri Lanka and Bangladesh from high energy prices and economic contraction in the Middle East
and beyond is likely to be significant. Notwithstanding a reduction in hostilities and even an improvement
in energy flows, we anticipate a period of economic slowness that will impact the business of CAL, and the
markets we operate in, for a few quarters to come.

Despite challenges on the trading front, the underlying fee-based business operations of CAL remain robust
and strong. CAL’s asset management business continues to grow apace both in Sri Lanka and Bangladesh.
Stockbroking is entering a period of rapid growth in Bangladesh and consolidation in Sri Lanka. The macro
fundamentals in Bangladesh, on stockbroking in particular, are promising. The market gears for significant
growth off a historically low base. Investment banking too, has seen a strong 12-month period with
historically high levels of deal volumes, and this looks set to continue in both markets. Several landmark
deals are in the pipeline for FY27 for both Sri Lanka and Bangladesh.

CAL’s strategic initiatives are also progressing well. Our customer reach through our online platform has
surpassed 80,000 users. AI is proving to be a significant productivity unlock across the board, but
particularly in the software development space-already resulting in a greater than 50% YoY increase in the
pace of new developments being rolled out in the quarter under review. Our teams are now working on
statistical macro modeling using AI and ML based technology. A breakthrough in this will result in a
dramatic improvement of CAL’s macro predictive capability, with the capacity for significant profits in the
years to come. This quarter marked the introduction of CAL’s first Sharia compliant product-a money
market unit trust fund. The market reaction to the fund has been positive, with close to LKR 1bn in AUM
within a short period of launching the fund. Several other products remain in the pipeline, ready to go to
market in FY27.

Despite short term headwinds, we remain confident in the capabilities we are building at CAL. The
medium to long term outlook for the business remains positive and strong.

  (Signed)
Kanishke Mannakkara
Group CEO/Director

      Accounting classification and fair value of financial instruments
      Financial instruments measured subsequently on the ongoing basis either at fair value or amortized cost. The summary of significant accounting policies

      describes how the classes of financial instruments are measured, and how income and expenses, including fair value gains and losses, are recognized.
      The group measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of inputs used in making the measurement.

      Level - 1
      Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial instrument is regarded as
      quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those prices represent actual and regularly occurring
      market transactions on an arm’s length basis.

      Level - 2
      Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or accessible for these
      securities, their fair value measures are determined using relevant information generated by market transactions involving comparable securities.

      Level - 3
      Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique includes inputs not
      based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument’s valuation. This category includes instruments that are
      valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant unobservable adjustments or assumptions are required to reflect differences
      between the instruments.
