  FINANCIAL COMMENTARY FOR THE PERIOD ENDED 30TH SEPTEMBER 2026
    •   Advances exceed Rs 300 Bn YTD growth while deposits increased by over Rs 250 Bn
    •   Net Interest Income rises 24% YoY, reflecting strong performance
    •   Asset quality strengthens, with Net Stage 3 ratio at 1.09%
    •   Liquidity and Capital Strength continue to support Growth

Amid shifting economic dynamics, Hatton National Bank PLC (HNB) recorded a solid financial
performance for the nine months ended September 2026. Group Profit After Tax (PAT) reached Rs
33.6 Bn, while the Bank recorded a PAT of Rs 33.0 Bn.

Balance sheet expansion during 9M 2026 was a key contributor to the Bank’s performance, with
Gross Advances recording a Year-to-Date (YTD) increase of Rs 313.5 Bn, equivalent to 20.7% growth.
This was complemented by Deposit growth of Rs 251.7 Bn, a 12.8% YTD. Amid a higher interest rate
environment, the Bank preserved a healthy CASA ratio of 32.0%, supporting a well-balanced funding
mix.

The Bank recorded a 23.6% YoY increase in Net Interest Income (NII) to Rs 85.5 Bn, while Net Interest
margin (NIM) stood at 4.50%. Strong lending momentum drove a 19.7% increase in interest income,
which translated into stronger NII growth as interest expenses rose at a comparatively lower rate of
16.6% despite policy-driven rate pressures.

A notable improvement in fee-based income further strengthened the Bank’s revenue profile, with
Net Fee and Commission income recording a 25.4% YoY increase to Rs 20.6 Bn. Increased card and
digital transactions, higher trade finance activity and growth in leasing and other services
contributed to the improved performance. Exchange-related income also recorded a notable
improvement during the period, reflecting gains from foreign currency position and revaluation
movements.

Strong growth across the Bank’s income streams translated into a 22.9% YoY increase in Total
Operating Income to Rs 112.7 Bn. Total Operating Expenses increased by 13.6%, primarily on account
of higher other operating expenses, partly attributable to investments in strategic transformation
initiatives. The cost-to-income ratio stood at a well-managed 36.1%.

Strong revenue growth and continued cost discipline drove a 28.8% YoY increase in Pre-impairment
Operating Profit to Rs 71.9 Bn, highlighting the quality of the Bank's earnings performance.

Growth in lending portfolio was accompanied by impairment provisioning of Rs 5.3 Bn, during the
period, compared to an impairment reversal of Rs 7.5 Bn in the corresponding period of the previous
year. The Bank maintained a Net Stage 3 ratio of 1.09% and a Stage 3 Coverage ratio of 73.46%,
highlighting the continued focus on managing credit risk and maintaining the quality of its loan
portfolio.

The Bank’s balance sheet continued to expand, with total assets reaching Rs 2.69 Tn as at
September 2026, representing a YTD growth of 12.7%. Gross loans and advances stood at Rs 1.82
Tn, while the Deposit base reached Rs 2.21 Tn. HNB also maintained sound capital and liquidity
positions, with a Tier I Capital Adequacy Ratio of 14.26%, a Total Capital Adequacy Ratio of 16.59%
and an All Currency Liquidity Coverage Ratio of 165.32%. This provides the Bank with sufficient
headroom to support future business growth while remaining comfortably within regulatory
requirements.

Rated AA-(lka) by Fitch Ratings Lanka Limited, HNB continued to receive recognition for its
performance across key business segments. During the period, the Bank was named “Best Bank for
Large Corporates in Sri Lanka 2026” by Euromoney, “Best Bank in Sri Lanka” by The Banker and “Sri
Lanka’s Strongest Bank” by The Asian Banker. HNB also received The Asian Banker’s “Best Retail
Bank in Sri Lanka” award for the 16th time, alongside recognition as “Best Retail Bank” and “Best SME
Bank” by Global Business Magazine for 2026. The Bank was also named “Sri Lanka’s Best Corporate
Citizen 2025” by the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce.

                                                         FAIR VALUE DISCLOSURE OF FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS

Fair value Disclosure of Financial Instruments
The Group measures the fair values of financial instruments using the following fair value hierarchy, which reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the
measurements.

Level 1: Fair value measurement using unadjusted quoted market price

When available, the fair values of financial instruments are determined using quoted market prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical instruments. A market
is regarded as active if quoted prices are readily and regularly available and represent actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm’s length basis.
Accordingly, the fair values of treasury bills and bonds have been derived using the market yields and market prices published by Central Bank of Sri Lanka while fair
value of quoted equity securities and PDI Bonds have been valued using the quoted market prices as at the reporting date


Level 2: Fair value measurement using significant observable inputs

In the absence of an active market for a financial instrument, the fair value is determined using quoted market prices in active markets for similar instruments or
quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered less than active or other valuation techniques in which all significant inputs are
directly or indirectly observable from market data. The spot and forward premiums available as at the reporting date have been used to estimate the fair value of
derivative financial instruments while the fair value of unquoted units have been measured using manager’s selling prices. The fair values of financial assets and
financial liabilities carried at amortised cost have been estimated by comparing the interest rates when they were first recognised with the current market rates of
similar instruments.The Bank classified the fair value of USD Step up Bond & PDI Bond under Level 2.


Level 3: Fair value measurement using significant unobservable inputs
Financial instruments for which the valuation technique includes inputs that are not observable and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the
instrument’s valuation.
There are no material changes in the fair value of financial assets categorised under Level 3 compared to the values reported as at 31st December 2025. The Group
has not changed the valuation models and assumptions used to measure the fair values of Level 3 financial instruments during the period ended 30th September
2026.
