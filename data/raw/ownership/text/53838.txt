DIRECTOR/CEO’S REVIEW


  First Capital Treasuries PLC (the Company) recorded a Profit after Tax of Rs. 204 million for the second quarter
  of FY 2026/27, reflecting a return to quarterly profitability following the challenging market conditions
  experienced during the first quarter. Consequently, the Company's Loss after Tax for the six months ended 30
  September 2026 narrowed to Rs. 537 million, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 1.57 billion in the
  corresponding period of the previous financial year.

  The performance for the first half of the year was shaped by significant shifts in monetary policy and pressure
  on government securities yields. A 100-basis-point increase in the monetary policy rate in May 2026, driven by
  inflationary pressures linked to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, contributed to volatility in the fixed-
  income market and affected the Company's trading performance.

  The Company recorded a trading loss of Rs. 1.69 billion on government securities during 1st half of FY 2026/27,
  compared to a trading gain of Rs. 1.81 billion in the corresponding period of the previous year. Net Interest
  Income moderated to Rs. 687 million from Rs. 988 million in 1st half of FY 2025/26. These developments were
  the primary factors influencing the Company's financial performance during the period.

  Notwithstanding the impact on earnings, the Company's balance sheet continued to reflect its commitment to
  its core business. Total investments in Government Securities increased to Rs. 78.30 billion as at 30 September
  2026, from Rs. 53.60 billion at the beginning of the financial year. Total net assets stood at Rs. 7.11 billion,
  compared to Rs. 7.65 billion at the commencement of FY 2026/27.

  The return to quarterly profitability in the second quarter provides a positive development within the reporting
  period. While recognising the impact of prevailing market conditions on the six-month results, we remain
  focused on maintaining a disciplined approach to portfolio management, strengthening risk controls and
  preserving the Company's financial position. We will continue to closely assess market developments and
  position our portfolios prudently, with a clear focus on managing market exposure and responding to
  opportunities as conditions evolve.

  Looking ahead, our priorities remain firmly anchored in our core business, with an emphasis on financial
  discipline, prudent risk management and operational efficiency. We remain confident in the Company's
  institutional capabilities and its ability to navigate changing market cycles while continuing to serve the evolving
  needs of the market.

  Our focus remains on building on our core strengths, safeguarding the Company's financial position and
  reinforcing its standing as a leading participant in Sri Lanka's government securities market. With disciplined
  execution and a long-term perspective, we remain committed to navigating current market conditions and
  creating sustainable value for our stakeholders.

 7   Financial Instruments- Fair Value Measurement
     The Company measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in making
     the measurements in accordance with SLFRS 13.

     Level - 1
     Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial
     instrument is regarded as quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those
     prices represent
     actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm's length basis.

     Level - 2
     Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or
     accessible for these securities, their fair value measures are determined using relevant information generated by market transactions
     involving comparable securities.

     Level - 3
     Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique
     includes inputs not based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument's valuation.
     This category includes instruments that are valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant unobservable
     adjustments or assumptions are required to reflect differences between the instruments.
