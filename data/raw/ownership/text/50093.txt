                      Financial review for the year ended 31 December 2025


Dialog Finance PLC reported a strong financial performance for the year ended 31 December 2025,
recording a Net Profit After Tax (NPAT) of Rs. 159.7 million, marking a significant turnaround from a loss of
Rs. 41.1 million in the previous year. Operating income surged by 107% year-on-year to Rs. 2.3 billion,
reflecting the Company’s continued focus on scalable, technology-led financial solutions.

Profit before taxes rose sharply to Rs. 437.5 million, an increase of 893% compared to the previous year,
driven by strong core business momentum, disciplined cost management, and improved margins. The
growth in operating income was largely underpinned by strategically structured fee income from key growth
engines such as Device Financing and the payments business, supported by steady improvements in asset
quality and net interest margins.

Total assets expanded by 62% year-on-year to Rs. 11.9 billion, while the net loan book grew by a robust 77%
to Rs. 9.1 billion, continuing to form the backbone of the Company’s earnings growth. Net Interest Income
increased by 72% to Rs. 1.3 billion, supported by prudent pricing strategies and effective asset-liability
management, resulting in a healthy Net Interest Margin of 15.01%.

Operating expenses grew at a moderated pace of 64%, leading to a notable improvement in the cost-to-
income ratio to 69% from 86% in the previous year. This reflects sustained efforts to enhance operational
efficiency while scaling the business. Impairment charges increased to Rs. 307.8 million, in line with the
expansion of the loan portfolio. Despite this, asset quality remained sound, with Stage 3 advances contained
at 4.94% and a strong coverage ratio of 73.33%, remaining well above industry benchmarks.

The Company’s capital position remained strong, with both Tier I and Total Capital Adequacy Ratios reported
at 17.03%, comfortably above the minimum regulatory requirements, reinforcing balance sheet resilience.

During the year, Dialog Finance continued to strengthen its digital ecosystem with several key developments.
The Company introduced doorstep delivery for mobile phones purchased via its LesiPay instalment platform,
enhancing customer convenience. Genie Business digital payment acceptance solutions were further
expanded, now coupled with guaranteed business loans to support MSME growth. In addition, a strategic
partnership with global payment network UnionPay enabled the expansion of innovative payment solutions
with nationwide acceptance and world-class digital payment experiences for Sri Lankan consumers and
merchants.

Dialog Finance PLC, a subsidiary of Dialog Axiata PLC, is a registered finance company in Sri Lanka
offering innovative, technology-driven financial services, digital lending, and payment solutions. The
Company is rated ‘AA(lka)’ by Fitch Ratings with a stable outlook.

4    Other Assets

     The Company is continuing the recovery efforts on case relating to external financial fraud that occurred in
     June 2024. As at the reporting date, the gross outstanding amount to be recovered was LKR 37.7Mn and based
     on an assessment of recoverability, the Company has recognized a provision for loss of LKR 25.6Mn and LKR
     8.3Mn in the statements of profit or loss in 2024 and 2025 respectively. Consequently, the net amount
     outstanding to be recovered as of the reporting date is LKR 3.7Mn, which is reported under "other assets" in
     the statement of financial position.

     The Company is continuing the legal action and working with law enforcement authorities to recover the
     remaining funds. As of the reporting date, the investigation and recovery efforts are ongoing. The Company will
     continue to monitor the progress of these actions and will assess the recoverability of the outstanding
     receivables recognised at each reporting date.

5    Fair value of financial instruments

     The Company uses valuation techniques that are appropriate in the circumstances and for which sufficient data
     are available to measure fair value, maximizing the use of relevant observable inputs and minimizing the use of
     unobservable inputs.
     All assets and liabilities for which fair value is measured or disclosed in the financial statements are categorized
     within the fair value hierarchy, described as follows, based on the lowest level input that is significant to the fair
     value measurement as a whole:

     Level 1 - Quoted (unadjusted) market prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities

     Level 2 - Valuation techniques for which the lowest level input that is significant to the fair value measurement is
     directly or indirectly observable

     Level 3 - Valuation techniques for which the lowest level input that is significant to the fair value measurement is
     unobservable

     For assets and liabilities that are recognized in the financial statements on a recurring basis, the Company
     determines whether transfers have occurred between levels in the hierarchy by re-assessing categorization (based
     on the lowest level input that is significant to the fair value measurement as a whole) at the end of each reporting
     period.
