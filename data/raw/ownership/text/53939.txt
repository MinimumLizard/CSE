JXG – Executive Summary
Six months ended 30 September 2026 (April – September 2026)


Consolidated Overview

Janashakthi Group (JXG) recorded consolidated revenue of LKR 13.0Bn for the six months ended
30 September 2026, compared to LKR 14.5Bn in the corresponding period last year. The Group
reported a Loss After Tax of LKR 1.5Bn, compared to a Profit After Tax of LKR 3.1Bn in the
comparative period.

The first half of FY27 was characterized by a markedly different operating environment, with supply-
side shocks arising from the Middle East conflict contributing to an increase in monetary policy
rates, inflationary pressure and heightened volatility across capital markets. While the Group's core
businesses continued to demonstrate operational resilience, including strong premium growth in the
insurance sector, adverse market movements and lower investment income impacted earnings
across the portfolio. The effect was most pronounced at First Capital Treasuries PLC, the Group's
primary dealer unit, which recorded a loss of LKR 537Mn. Although the impact was most acute
during the first quarter, performance improved sequentially in the second quarter, with consolidated
revenue increasing 3% Quarter-on-Quarter and losses narrowing as market conditions showed
signs of stabilization.

Subsidiary Performance

Market volatility continued to weigh on the performance of First Capital Holdings PLC, which
recorded a Loss After Tax of LKR 1.3Bn for the six months ended 30 September 2026, compared to
a Net Profit After Tax (NPAT) of LKR 3.4Bn in the corresponding period last year. While its primary
dealing, corporate dealing and wealth management businesses were most impacted by market
reversals, corporate finance advisory and stockbroking continued to deliver positive results.

A similar impact was reflected in Janashakthi Insurance PLC, where strong underlying business
growth was offset by investment market volatility. Gross Written Premiums grew by 21% year-on-
year to LKR 7.4Bn for the nine months ended 30 September 2026, supported by 14% growth in new
business premiums. However, mark-to-market movements weighed on profitability, with NPAT
declining to LKR 158Mn from LKR 2.8Bn in the corresponding period last year.

In contrast, Janashakthi Finance PLC demonstrated resilience, benefiting from its continued focus
on business growth and value creation. Janashakthi Finance recorded a 19.5% increase in NPAT to
LKR 162Mn for the six months ended 30 September 2026, supported by a 11% growth in Net
Operating Income to LKR 1.59 billion. Its loan book and deposit base also strengthened, reaching
LKR 32.9Bn and LKR 19.4Bn, respectively.



Strategic Progress and Outlook
At the holding-company level, our focus remains firmly on delivering the strategic commitments
outlined at the time of Janashakthi PLC's Initial Public Offering (IPO). The signing of the Share
Purchase Agreement to acquire Continental Insurance Lanka Limited marks an important step
towards the Group's planned re-entry into the general insurance sector. Upon obtaining approval from
the Insurance Regulatory Commission of Sri Lanka, the acquisition is expected to broaden the

Group's insurance footprint and further strengthen its presence across Sri Lanka's financial services
landscape.

In parallel, we continue to advance our strategic ambitions in microfinance and explore opportunities
for overseas expansion. With a dedicated CEO now in place to lead the microfinance business and a
dedicated officer appointed to drive international growth initiatives, groundwork is underway across
both fronts, with preliminary market engagements and exploratory visits already commenced. These
initiatives are intended to create additional avenues for growth, broaden the Group's business
capabilities and strengthen its long-term competitive position.

Looking ahead to the next six months, Sri Lanka's economic outlook remains broadly stable, with
GDP growth projected at 4-5%. However, inflation is expected to remain above the CBSL's 5% target
until after Q1 2027, limiting the prospect of near-term interest rate reductions. We recognize that
market pressures may continue to weigh on investment valuations and claims cost. However, the
group and its subsidiaries are strongly capitalized to absorb these uncertainties. We are positive in
our outlook and remain committed to our business lines.

5.4. Determination of fair value and fair value hierarchy

    The Group measures fair values using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in making the measurements in accordance with SLFRS 13.

    Level - 1
    Financial instruments that are measured in whole or in part by reference to published quotes in an active market. A financial instrument is regarded as quoted in an active market if quoted
    prices are readily and regularly available from an exchange and those prices represent actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm's length basis.

    Level - 2
    Financial instruments that are measured at fair value on a recurring basis. As market quotes generally are not readily available or accessible for these securities, their fair value measures are
    determined using relevant information generated by market transactions involving comparable securities.

    Level - 3
    Valuation techniques using significant unobservable inputs. This category includes all instruments where the valuation technique includes inputs not based on observable data and the
    unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument's valuation. This category includes instruments that are valued based on quoted prices for similar instruments where significant
    unobservable adjustments or assumptions are required to reflect differences between the instruments.
