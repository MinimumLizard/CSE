Hemas Holdings PLC | Interim Report 2026/27 - Q2
Performance Review for the Six Months Ended 30th September
2026

We are pleased to report a strong second quarter, marking a clear recovery from a challenging first
quarter. Q2 FY27 revenue grew 17.0% YoY to LKR 37.8 Bn, taking H1 FY27 revenue to LKR 66.6
Bn, up 9.5% YoY. EBITDA rebounded sharply to LKR 4.5 Bn, nearly double the Q1 level, driving a
more than twofold increase in Group Earnings to LKR 2.0 Bn. We chose to absorb much of the
higher input, energy and currency costs arising from the Middle East war rather than pass the full
burden on to consumers, while disciplined cost management supported profitability during the
quarter.
Nearly half of Group revenue is derived from businesses subject to regulated pricing, which limits
our ability to recover cost increases through price corrections. In the consumer business, we have
implemented pricing corrections judiciously to ensure that volumes continue to grow. Experience
has shown that volume erosion costs a business far more over the long term than a temporary
adjustment to margins during a period of high inflation. Our approach, particularly for mass market
consumers, has therefore been to leverage technology and innovation to lower our cost base, by
accessing global technologies to reformulate our products and by increasingly automating our
factories to reduce manufacturing costs.
On the strategic front, the Group completed its first international acquisition on 17 August 2026,
acquiring a 75% stake in Twiga Stationers & Printers Limited, Kenya. As a leading stationery
manufacturer and exporter in East Africa, Twiga strengthens the Group's position as a leading
learning solutions provider while establishing a platform for long term growth across the region.
Economic indicators presented a mixed picture during the quarter. Average inflation, as measured
by the Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), rose to 7.7%, with nearly 80% of the increase
attributable to higher food, electricity, gas and fuel prices. While the LKR appreciated 1.7% against
the USD during the quarter, it remains 5.0% weaker on a YTD basis, continuing to exert pressure
on imported input costs.
Brent crude prices moderated from their April peak to a low in July before rebounding as tensions
in the Middle East intensified. Local fuel prices followed a similar trajectory, with LP92 declining
before returning to LKR 414 per litre, while LAD increased by LKR 10 to LKR 392 per litre during
the quarter. Electricity tariffs remained unchanged; however, both fuel and power costs remained
elevated relative to the beginning of the financial year.


Financial and Operating Performance
In Q2 FY27, Group revenue increased 17.0% YoY to LKR 37.8 Bn, with all four sectors contributing
to growth, led by Hospitals (+19.3%) and Consumer Brands (+18.3%). The quarter marked a strong
recovery from Q1: revenue rose 31.4% QoQ, EBITDA nearly doubled to LKR 4.5 Bn with margin
up 4.1 pp QoQ to 11.9%, and Group Earnings increasing 113.4% to LKR 2.0 Bn.

Compared with Q2 FY26, gross margin remained under pressure, declining 3.1 pp to 29.7%, a
further 0.7 pp lower than Q1. Tighter cost management and operating leverage kept selling,
distribution and administrative expenses growing slower than revenue, enabling EBITDA to grow
10.5% YoY with margin only 0.7 pp lower. Performance was mixed across sectors, with margin
expansions in Hospitals and Mobility partly offsetting lower earnings in Consumer Brands and Life

For H1 FY27, revenue grew 9.5% YoY to LKR 66.6 Bn. The strong second quarter, which delivered

While profitability recovered strongly in Q2, cash generation was constrained by higher working
capital requirements. The Group recorded an operating cash outflow of LKR 8.4 Bn in H1 FY27,
driven primarily by an LKR 12.3 Bn increase in net working capital to support revenue growth, a
seasonal inventory build-up at Atlas and Twiga, and higher receivables from extended distributor
payment terms in Consumer Brands and Pharmaceuticals. These working capital requirements,
together with funding for the Twiga acquisition, contributed to net gearing increasing to 16.6%,
compared with a net cash position in the corresponding period last year. Management is
prioritising tighter inventory management, improved collections and tighter distributor credit
control to strengthen cash conversion.

Home and Personal Care - Sri Lanka
During the quarter, Beauty recorded the strongest growth within Hemas’ HPC portfolio, followed
by Home Care and Baby Care, with all three categories achieving double digit growth. Beauty, a
strategic priority, now accounts for 20% of the portfolio. Baby Care growth was volume led amid
lower prices, while growth in Beauty and Home Care was predominantly price led, supported by
single digit volume growth. Across the broader portfolio, new product launches and promotional
initiatives contributed to improved volume performance. Feminine hygiene was the only category
to record a continued volume decline, due to lower Thilinapath1 contributions compared with Q2
FY26. Home Care’s Q2 performance was encouraging following a weak Q1, although YTD revenue
remains at a low single digit decline.
Gross margin erosion remained the key challenge, diluting EBITDA despite revenue growth. Higher
raw material, energy and logistics costs reduced gross margin by 5.6 pp QoQ and 3.6 pp YoY.
Higher overheads continued to weigh on profitability, although this was partially offset by a
modest reduction in direct marketing expenditure. While finance income increased marginally, a
higher tax charge more than offset this benefit, resulting in earnings declining broadly in line with
EBITDA.
We adopted a balanced pricing approach during the period, absorbing a portion of the increase in
input costs to protect long-term volumes and consumer affordability. Margin improvement
continues to be driven through supply chain and manufacturing efficiencies, supported by the
development of cost-effective advanced formulations. These efforts are further complemented by
ongoing investments in digital capabilities, with several initiatives being rolled out during FY27 to
enhance demand planning, commodity forecasting and overall operational effectiveness.


Home and Personal Care - Bangladesh
In HCB Bangladesh, value-added hair oil, which accounts for 74% of revenue, recovered YoY in
the quarter ended September 2026, as price growth outweighed a volume decline to deliver single-
digit growth. The male grooming category grew by nearly 50%, with double-digit growth in both
volume and price. Revenue recorded single-digit growth in both the quarter and the YTD periods
despite a challenging macroeconomic environment, while YTD earnings margin improved by a
modest 0.5%.
Innovation and new category launches continue under our “Build for Bangladesh” strategy, with a
focus on herbal and natural products aligned with local consumer preferences.


Learning
We continue our transformation into an activity-based learning company, supporting national
education reforms and the shift towards experiential learning. The Edutoys category recorded
significant growth in both the quarter and the YTD period, and its range was further strengthened
by the launch of seven activity kits during H1 FY27.
Market leadership across our core categories remains firmly established, while newer adjacencies
continue to gain traction. The Back-to-School and Bags categories, which now account for over
10% of the portfolio, recorded volume growth in excess of 50% YoY.

Pharmaceutical Distribution
Industry growth remained subdued, with the market recording a modest 0.5% QoQ expansion in
the quarter ended June 2026. Hemas Pharmaceuticals sustained its market leadership and secured
six new product registrations during the quarter, taking the YTD total to 14.
With pharmaceutical prices regulated by the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA),
the industry continued to absorb the full impact of LKR depreciation pending regulatory approval
of price revisions to partially offset the increased costs. Consequently, despite YoY revenue
growth, margins remained under pressure, while elevated financing costs further weighed on
earnings.
Challenges surrounding the NMRA pricing mechanism and approval delays, and their implications
for the timely availability of medicines, persist. While the stay order on the pricing formula,
effective 14 October, has provided temporary relief, the Group will continue to assess pricing
decisions on a case-by-case basis, balancing the need to safeguard medicine affordability with
ensuring sustainable market availability.


Pharmaceutical Manufacturing
Pharmaceutical manufacturing revenue grew 11% YoY in Q2 and a more modest 3% YTD, driven
entirely by volumes, as prices declined 1% YoY in the quarter and remained flat YTD. Morison’s
own brand portfolio continued to perform strongly, with volumes up 23% YoY in Q2 and 33%
YTD.

People, Culture & Future-Ready Skills
Following the rollout of the updated Group Values in August, Hemas launched “Move with
Courage”, a company-wide people and culture initiative to align all employees behind our long-
term growth ambitions and strategic priorities. Move with Courage translates the Group Long
Range Plan 2030 (LRP) into everyday action by strengthening four critical behaviours, namely
speed, ownership, ambition and collaboration, that are essential to achieving our LRP goals and
sharpening our execution.
Leadership development momentum continued across the Group. The third cohort of Transcend,
our flagship leadership development programme for senior managers, was launched. Transcend
builds future-ready leaders by strengthening strategic thinking, people leadership and an
enterprise-wide perspective. The third cohort of ElevateHER, our flagship women’s leadership
programme, is progressing through workshops and coaching. For the third consecutive year,
Hemas was recognised among the Top 20 Most Outstanding Women-Friendly Workplaces,
reflecting our continued commitment to building an inclusive workplace. Through LinkedIn
Learning, we are curating learning paths for four priority future skills (Strategic Thinking, Project
Management, AI and Change Management), supported by engagement initiatives to drive uptake.


Sustainability
The Group continued to advance its sustainability priorities during the quarter, embedding
sustainability considerations into business operations while enhancing long-term resilience and
regulatory preparedness.
Cumulative plastic recovery exceeded 3.5 million kilograms, supporting the Group’s targets of
recovering 62.5% of plastic placed on the market by FY27 and achieving 100% recovery by FY30.
These efforts also contribute to the Group’s preparedness for Sri Lanka’s forthcoming Extended
Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations.
Water intensity improved to 1.5 m³ from 1.6 m³ in the previous quarter, reflecting improved water
efficiency across operations. Targeted conservation measures and process optimisation initiatives
continue to support resource efficiency.
Renewable energy accounted for 14.8% of total electricity consumption, compared with 13.6% in
the previous quarter. This improvement reflects progress towards the Group’s target of sourcing
25% of its electricity requirements from renewable energy.
During the quarter, the Group’s purpose-led initiatives reached over 24,300 individuals across
education, health and wellbeing, and community development. The Piyawara preschool network
expanded access to quality early childhood education through the opening of five new preschools
in Tissamaharama, Nawagampura, Kohombagasthalawa, Passara, and Hali-Ela, bringing its total
network to 82 centres.

Strategy and Outlook
Q2 FY27 demonstrated the resilience of Hemas in a challenging environment. In a period shaped
by the Middle East war, higher energy costs and a volatile currency, our teams protected
consumers, ensured the continued availability of medicines and delivered a strong recovery in
profitability from the first quarter. This resilience is the foundation on which we are building our
next chapter.
Our Long Range Plan sets clear north stars: mid-teen compound annual growth in both revenue
and earnings, with 15% of revenue to be derived from international markets by FY30. To achieve
this, we have earmarked over USD 100 Mn for new growth platforms. We will deploy this capital
with discipline, prioritising opportunities that build on our strengths, offer scalable growth and
meet clear return thresholds.
Twiga represents our next step on the international journey. As it enters its school season, our
priority is to deliver Twiga’s plan and develop it into a platform for growth across East Africa,
reducing our dependence on a single economy and extending our learning expertise to many more
young learners.
In Sri Lanka, we expect the third stage of the public sector salary revision, amounting to LKR 110
Bn in 2027, to support affordability and volumes despite elevated inflation. Together with our
relentless focus on cost and innovation, this gives us confidence in the momentum we carry into
the second half.
Above all, we will continue to Move with Courage. With a clear strategy, trusted brands and
talented people, we are determined to convert this quarter’s recovery into sustained growth and
lasting value for our shareholders, consumers and the communities we serve.
