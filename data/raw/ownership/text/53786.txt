Performance Review for the six months ended 30th September 2026

During the first half of FY2027, Sri Lanka’s tourism sector faced a challenging operating environment. In the first
quarter, escalating conflict in the Middle East disrupted global aviation through intermittent regional airspace
closures and uncertainty affecting key European long-haul feeder routes. Conditions improved in the second
quarter, though arrivals remained below the previous year’s levels. A pick in arrivals from India further
strengthened its status as the principal source market, followed by China and Australia, which helped partially
offset the degrowth in the Western markets. Further, spending per visitor remained below the level recorded in
the corresponding period of the previous year. In light of these challenges, the Government revised its 2026
tourism targets to 2.5 million arrivals and USD 4.2 Bn in earnings, down from earlier targets of 3.0 million arrivals
and USD 5.0 Bn in earnings. Meanwhile, pressure from modern Colombo-based hotels, informal accommodation
providers and purpose-built banquet venues competing for the same limited market continued.

The Hotel’s performance during the period reflects the benefits of the asset enhancement and repositioning
programme initiated in FY2026, as it continues its transition towards a city beach resort offering. The fully
operational ‘Sand and Spice’ restaurant and ‘Salt Theory’ beach bar have enhanced the guest experience and
broadened the Hotel’s leisure appeal. Occupancy improved to 61% in 1HFY27, from 51% in the corresponding
period, supported by the availability of the full room inventory following the completion of refurbishments,
together with growth in the conference and online segments. Targeted pricing for the local market also helped
sustain volumes in a competitive operating environment. The MICE segment maintained its momentum,
underpinned by several large-scale corporate events. The Wedding segment, however, remained subdued amid
continued competition from standalone banquet venues. Accordingly, Pegasus Hotels of Ceylon PLC recorded
revenue of Rs. 510 Mn for the six months ended 30th September 2026, compared to Rs. 403 Mn in the
corresponding period of the previous year, an increase of 26.6%.

Direct costs increased in line with higher business volumes, while the upward revision of electricity tariffs placed
further pressure on the cost base. Maintenance expenses declined YoY following the completion of several major
maintenance projects in FY2026. Overheads increased as the Hotel continued investing in its people and brand,
including higher marketing expenditure to sustain visibility in a highly competitive market. Net finance income
improved, mainly as higher market interest rates boosted returns on deposits while the Hotel’s borrowings
remained at fixed rates. As a result, the loss before tax for 1HFY27 narrowed to Rs. 13.6 Mn, compared to Rs. 53.0
Mn in 1HFY26.

The Hotel’s next refurbishment project, an upgrade of the main banquet hall, is scheduled to begin in the latter
part of the financial year. While near-term uncertainties persist, the outlook for the industry remains positive,
supported by improving tourist arrivals and resilient regional demand. The Hotel will continue to focus on
delivering exceptional value to guests while strengthening service standards and operational excellence across
all touchpoints.
