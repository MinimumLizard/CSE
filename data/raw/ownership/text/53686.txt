Financial Review for the nine months ended September 30, 2026

  Consistency the key as ComBank Group posts 9-month topline of Rs.
                            318.8 billion

    ▪   Loan book grows by Rs. 348.4 Bn. in 9 months to Rs. 2.43 Tn.
    ▪   Total assets up by Rs. 496.1 Bn. to Rs 3.87 Tn.
    ▪   Bank takes its deposits past Rs 3 Tn. following Group milestone in preceding
        quarter
    ▪   Continues prudent provisioning for impairment in readiness for external
        pressures on local businesses


The Commercial Bank of Ceylon Group has posted consistent growth in business volumes and its balance
sheet in the third quarter of 2026 to end the first nine months of the year in a characteristically strong
position.


Comprising Sri Lanka’s largest private sector bank and six subsidiaries, the Group reported gross income
of Rs. 318.79 billion for the nine months ending 30th September, an increase of 18.87%, with interest
income for the period improving by 20.52% to Rs. 266.98 billion. Gross income for the third quarter alone
was up 20.01% to Rs. 109.63 billion, primarily due to the healthy growth of the loan book.


A combination of rising interest rates and notable growth in deposits resulted in interest expense for the
nine months growing by 23.62% to Rs. 145.92 billion. Consequently, the Group posted a net interest
income of Rs. 121.06 billion for the nine months and Rs. 41.36 billion for the third quarter, reflecting
growth of 16.98% and 19.30% respectively.


Total operating income for the nine months grew by 14.37% to Rs. 160.33 billion, and the Group reported
it had increased its provisions for impairment charges and other losses by 17.84% to Rs. 16.57 billion for
the nine months as a prudential measure, resulting in net operating income of Rs. 143.76 billion for the
period, an improvement of 13.98%.
Operating expenses increased by 19.94% to Rs. 47.27 billion for the nine months on account of increased
staff costs, technology investments and related expenses, leading to an operating profit before taxes on
financial services of Rs. 96.49 billion for the period reviewed, a growth of 11.27%.


With taxes on financial services for the period increasing by 25.35% to Rs. 16.75 billion, the Group
reported a profit before income tax of Rs. 79.74 billion, reflecting a growth of 8.71%. Income tax for the
period increased by 8.86% to Rs. 27.57 billion, resulting in a nine-month net profit after tax of
Rs. 52.16 billion for the Group, a growth of 8.63%. Higher provisioning for impairment and increased
operating expenses impacted net profit growth.


The Group reported a net profit of Rs. 16.74 billion for the third quarter, compared to Rs. 16.86 billion in
the corresponding quarter of last year, reflecting a marginal decline of 0.68%.


The Bank posted nine-month pre-tax profit of Rs. 76.16 billion and net profit of Rs. 49.54 billion, up 7.92%
and 7.64% respectively.


The Group’s gross loans and advances grew by 16.70% or Rs. 348.37 billion over the nine months at an
average of Rs. 38.71 billion per month, taking the loan book to Rs. 2.43 trillion as at 30th September 2026,
underscoring the scale of the Bank’s role as a lender. Loan book growth over the preceding 12 months
amounted to Rs. 526.88 billion or 27.63% at a monthly average of Rs. 43.91 billion.


Meanwhile, deposits at Group level grew by Rs. 418.98 billion or 15.52% in the nine months ending 30th
September 2026, at a monthly average of Rs. 46.55 billion, to reach Rs. 3.12 trillion. Over the preceding
12 months, deposits grew by a remarkable Rs. 530.30 billion or 20.49% at a monthly average of Rs. 44.19
billion. Taken separately, Commercial Bank’s deposits crossed Rs. 3 trillion for the first time, during the
quarter.


Total assets of the Group grew by a noteworthy 14.68% or Rs. 496.08 billion since December 2025 to Rs.
3.87 trillion as at 30th September 2026, and by an even more impressive Rs. 642.26 billion or 19.87% over
the preceding 12 months. This represents a monthly average growth of Rs. 55.12 billion over the nine
months reviewed, and Rs. 53.52 billion a month over the preceding year.
In terms of key performance ratios, the Bank’s Tier 1 Capital Ratio as of 30th September 2026 stood at
12.50%, compared to 13.03% at the end of 2025, while its Total Capital Ratio stood at 16.35% as against
16.69% as at 31st December 2025. The ratios remained significantly higher than the regulatory minimum
ratios of 10% and 14% respectively, but were marginally lower than those reported in December 2025 due
to the substantial growth in assets and the fact that the profit of the third quarter was not taken into
consideration when computing the ratios for the review period.


In terms of profitability, the Bank’s net interest margin for the period declined to 4.45% (annualised) from
4.51% at the end of 2025, a marginal reduction attributable to the increased cost of deposits. The Bank’s
return on assets (before tax) was 2.92% (annualised) compared to 2.96% at the end of 2025, while the
return on equity (after tax - annualised) was 19.31% compared to 19.51% at the end of 2025.


The Bank’s cost to income ratio excluding taxes on financial services stood at 29.24%, as against 29.66%
for 2025, while the figure inclusive of taxes on financial services was 40% for the nine months, in
comparison with 39.20% for the preceding year.


The CASA ratio of the Bank declined to 37.08% as at 30th September 2026, from 39.65% at the end of
2025, but continues to be one of the best in the industry.


In terms of asset quality, the Bank’s net impaired loans (Stage 3) to total loans ratio stood at 1.47% as at
30th September 2026 compared to 1.54% at the end of 2025 and 1.79% a year ago. The gross impaired
loans (Stage 3) to total loans ratio stood at 5.31% as against 5.81% at the end of 2025 and 6.25% at the
end of the third quarter of 2025. The Impairment (Stage 3) to Stage 3 loans ratio stood at 72.35% as at
30th September 2026, down from 73.50% at the end of 2025 but up from 71.43% a year ago.


The Bank's liquidity coverage ratio as at 30th September 2026 stood at 421.11% for Rupees and 226.57%
for all currencies, both well above the statutory minimum ratios of 100%. The Bank’s net stable funding
ratio stood at 163.10% as at 30th September 2026, also significantly higher than the minimum statutory
requirement of 100%.
Commercial Bank was the first Sri Lankan bank to achieve a market capitalisation exceeding US$ 1 billion.
The Bank emerged as the No. 1 ranked Sri Lankan bank in the 2026 edition of the prestigious Top 1000
World Banks ranking published by The Banker magazine, UK. The Bank has the highest capital base among
all Sri Lankan banks and is the largest private-sector lender in Sri Lanka. Ranked No. 1 in the Business
Today Top 40, Commercial Bank is recognised as the most respected and most-awarded bank in Sri Lanka,
is a leader in digital innovation and is the country’s first 100% carbon-neutral bank.


Commercial Bank operates with 274 strategically located branches and an extensive network of
automated machines island-wide, and has the widest international footprint among Sri Lankan banks,
with 21 branches in Bangladesh, a majority stake in a fully-fledged Tier I bank in the Maldives, a
microfinance company in Myanmar, and a representative office in the Dubai International Financial Centre
(DIFC). The Bank’s wholly owned subsidiaries, CBC Finance PLC and Commercial Insurance Brokers (Pvt)
Limited, also deliver a range of financial services via their own branch networks.

                              ASSETS & LIABILITIES MEASURED AT FAIR VALUE - FAIR VALUE HIERARCHY                                                                                  14

Level 1
Inputs that are quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an active market for identical instruments.

When available, the Group/Bank measures the fair value of an instrument using active quoted prices or dealer price quotations, without any deduction for transaction costs. A
market is regarded as active if transactions for asset or liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis.


Level 2
Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable either directly (i.e., as prices) or indirectly (i.e., derived from prices).
This category includes instruments valued using;

      (a) quoted prices in active markets for similar instruments,
      (b) quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered to be less active, or
      (c) other valuation techniques in which almost all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.


Level 3
Inputs that are unobservable.
This category includes all instruments for which the valuation technique includes inputs not based on observable data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on
the instrument’s valuation.
This category includes instruments that are valued based on quoted prices of similar instruments for which significant unobservable adjustments or assumptions are required to
reflect differences between the instruments.

Valuation techniques include net present value and discounted cash flow models, comparison with similar instruments for which observable market prices exist. Assumptions
and inputs used in valuation techniques include risk-free and benchmark interest rates, risk premiums in estimating discount rates, bond and equity prices, foreign exchange
rates, expected price volatilities and correlations.

There are no material changes in the fair value of financial assets categorised under Level 3 compared to the values reported as at December 31, 2025. The Bank continued to
use the valuation models and assumptions used to measure the fair values of Level 3 financial instruments as at December 31, 2025, during the period ended September 30,
2026, as well.

4   Quarterly Update on SLFRS Sustainability-related Financial Disclosures
    The Bank adopted the SLFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards, comprising SLFRS S1 General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information and SLFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures, with effect from January 01, 2025. The
    Bank’s complete sustainability-related financial disclosures for the year ended December 31, 2025 were included in the Annual Report 2025.
    This condensed interim update should be read in conjunction with the annual climate-related disclosures set out on pages 141-171 of the Bank’s 2025 Annual Report. It provides information about significant climate-related events, changes in
    circumstances and new information arising during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and does not repeat information that remains substantially unchanged.
    During the period, the Management reassessed the four climate-related risks and opportunities (CRROs)disclosed in the Annual Report 2025: borrower vulnerability to acute physical risk; borrower vulnerability to transition risk; reputational risk arising
    from an inadequate climate response; and sustainable finance opportunities. The Management also reassessed the principal assumptions underlying the related climate metrics. No new climate-related risk or opportunity was identified, and the nature,
    scope and time horizons of the four previously disclosed risks and opportunities remained unchanged.
    Looking ahead, the Bank has identified the El Niño event forecast for late 2026 and early 2027 as a development relevant to this risk. El Niño is a natural climate phenomenon whose effects are expected to intensify with climate change. It increases the
    likelihood of extreme weather events, such as heavy rainfall, drought and extreme heat, which may weaken borrowers' financial capacity. Forecasts by international climate agencies and the Department of Meteorology, Sri Lanka indicate that the current
    event is likely to bring a wet phase in November–December 2026, followed by a dry phase in February–March 2027. Based on the forecasts available in September 2026, the Bank carried out a preliminary assessment of the potential impact of the event on
    its loan portfolio in Sri Lanka. No related losses arose during the nine months ended September 30, 2026. However, the extent of any future impact remains uncertain, as it will depend on the strength of the event and on rainfall during both phases. The
    Bank continues to monitor these developments closely.
    Except for the updated assessment under CRRO1 concerning possible acute physical risks amplified by the El Niño cycle (including anticipated intensified drought and water stress in select operating regions, alongside localized severe storms and flash
    flooding), the Management did not identify any material change during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 to the CRROs, or to the related governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets disclosed in the Annual Report 2025.
