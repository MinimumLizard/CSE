               National Development Bank PLC (NDB/the Bank) announced its financial results for the nine
               months ended September 30, 2026 (9M 2026), as released to the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE)
               on October 21, 2026. The Bank delivered a strong performance during the period, underpinned by
               the resilience of its core banking franchise, disciplined execution of its strategic priorities, and the
               strength of its business model, despite the challenges arising from the fraud incident identified in
               April 2026.

               Final financial impact due to the fraud

               As per the disclosure released to the CSE on September 29, 2026, the final forensic report of the
               fraud incident identified by the Bank, had confirmed that the final fraud is a sum of circa LKR
               13.64 billion compared to the circa Rs.13.58 billion as announced via Corporate Disclosure on
               June 26, 2026 following the release of the interim forensic report. The incremental fraud amount
               has been recognized in accordance with applicable Accounting Standards. The gross impact of the
               fraud applicable for the 9M ended September 30, 2026 is LKR 2.6 billion whilst the applicable
               amount for the 9M ended September 30, 2025 is LKR 4.5 billion.

               Further details on the accounting treatment and recognition of the fraud-related impact are
               provided in Notes 3 and 10 to the financial statements.

               Resilient earnings performance continues to be supported by its sound core banking
               operations

               The Bank reported an operating profit before taxes on financial services of LKR 15.1 billion for
               9M 2026, after recognising the gross financial impact of the fraud attributable to the period
               amounting to LKR 2.6 billion, which related entirely to the quarter ended March 31, 2026. This

               2025, which has been restated to reflect the applicable fraud impact of LKR 4.5 billion recognised

               Post-tax profit for 9M 2026 amounted to LKR 7.7 billion, compared with a restated post-tax profit
               of LKR 5.1 billion for 9M 2025, with the net financial impact of the fraud reflected in both periods.
               Excluding the impact of the fraud, post-tax profit for 9M 2026 would have been LKR 9.1 billion,

               tax profit of LKR 2.9 billion in Q3 2026, compared with LKR 3.0 billion in Q2 2026 and LKR 1.8
               billion in Q1 2026. Excluding the fraud loss, Q1 2026 post-tax profit would have been LKR 3.2
               billion.

               Net banking revenue improves by 14.8% underpinned by further improved asset quality

               The Bank posted net banking revenue of LKR 32.6 billion, representing a 14.8% increase over the
               comparative period, driven by the strength of its core banking operations. This performance is
               reported before taking into account any financial impact arising from the fraud incident.

               Supporting this performance, total revenue increased by 10.6% YoY to LKR 80.6 billion. Net
               interest income (NII) was LKR 25.4 billion, with a marginal decline of 2.2% attributable to the
               prevailing low interest rate environment and the faster pace of deposits repricing. However, the
               downside of these factors were mitigated through prudent balance sheet management, disciplined
               pricing strategies, and effective asset and liability management. The resultant net interest margin
               (NIM) was 3.6%, compared with 4.1% for FY 2025.

               Net fee and commission income also expanded by 14.1% to LKR 6.6 billion supporting the
               diversification and resilience of the Bank’s revenue mix. Key drivers of net fee and commission
               income were credit and cards and related services. Other non-fund-based income, comprising gains
               from trading activities, financial assets measured at fair value through profit or loss, de-recognition
               of financial assets, and other operating income, amounted to LKR 3.7 billion during 9M 2026 (9M
               2025: LKR 2.6 billion). Other operating income included foreign reserve revaluation gains of LKR
               953.0 million, up from LKR 396.8 million in 9M 2025.

               Impairment charges on loans and other investments declined by 48.4% year on year to LKR 3.0
               billion, primarily reflecting the Bank's continued focus on asset quality management, enhanced
               credit underwriting standards, closer monitoring of asset quality and stage migration trends, and
               strengthened recovery efforts. The impaired loans (Stage 3) to Total Loans Ratio – Net, improved
               to 3.0% as at September 30, 2026 from 3.8% at end-2025, while Stage 3 provision coverage
               improved to 65.4% from 59.1%.

               Total operating expenses for 9M 2026 amounted to LKR 17.5 billion, including LKR 2.6 billion
               recognised under other operating expenses in relation to the fraud. The comparative operating
               expense for 9M 2025, inclusive of the fraud-related expense applicable to that period, was LKR
               18.3 billion.

               Balance Sheet remains strong and resilient. Net loans and deposits expand during Q3 2026

               The Bank’s total assets grew by 5.3% to LKR 975.4 billion as at September 30, 2026, after
               recognising the financial impact of the fraud, compared with a restated asset base of LKR 926.1
               billion as at December 31, 2025. On an unadjusted basis, total assets as at September 30, 2026
               would have amounted to LKR 986.5 billion, compared with LKR 935.8 billion at end-2025.

               Net loans increased to LKR 599.7 billion from LKR 593.6 billion as at December 31, 2025, in line
               with the Bank’s loan growth aspirations for the year, while total deposits grew to LKR 717.5 billion
               from LKR 707.2 billion. The Bank’s CASA ratio stood at 20.7% as at September 30, 2026,
               compared with 23.9% at end-2025, reflecting industry-wide pressure on low-cost deposits amid
               the evolving interest rate environment. Total equity attributable to shareholders amounted to LKR
               82.8 billion, while Group equity stood at LKR 90.1 billion as at September 30, 2026.

               Liquidity and solvency continue to remain above the regulatory requirements

               The Bank’s liquidity and capital position remain sound throughout the period under review.
               Liquidity Coverage Ratios (LCR) in rupee and all-currency terms stood at 199.0% and 161.8%,
               respectively, while the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) was 126.8%. All ratios remained
               comfortably above the regulatory minimum requirement of 100.0%.

               The Bank also maintained healthy solvency levels, with Common Equity Tier 1 (CET 1)/Tier I
               Capital and Total Capital Adequacy Ratios (CAR) of 9.5% and 14.8%, respectively, as at
               September 30, 2026, remaining above applicable regulatory requirements. The corresponding
               restated ratios as at December 31, 2025 were 11.3% and 14.8%, respectively.

               Investor Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

               All KPIs for 9M 2026 are presented after incorporating the financial impact of the fraud, with
               comparative figures similarly restated. Return on Average Equity (ROE) improved to 12.9% for
               9M 2026, compared with a restated ROE of 7.5% for FY 2025. Pre-tax Return on Average Assets
               (ROA) was 2.2%, compared with a restated 1.4% for FY 2025.

               Annualised Earnings per Share (EPS) increased to LKR 24.23 from a restated LKR 13.83 for FY
               2025. At Group level, ROE and EPS stood at 11.8% and LKR 23.90, respectively, compared with
               restated FY 2025 figures of 8.4% and LKR 15.77. Net Asset Value (NAV) per share stood at LKR
               191.48 as at September 30, 2026 compared with a restated LKR 187.36 as at December 31, 2025.
               Group NAV per share was LKR 204.98 compared with a restated LKR 201.31 at end-2025. Share
               price closed at LKR 109.50 (FY 2025: LKR 141.25).

               Commenting on the Bank’s financial performance for the nine months period ended
               September 30, 2026, the Bank’s Director/ Chief Executive Officer, Mr. Kelum Edirisinghe,
               stated:

               “Despite the extraordinary circumstances faced, the Bank continues to demonstrate strength and
               resilience. Our Q3 2026 performance reflects the disciplined execution of our strategy to
               recalibrate the balance sheet and pursue measured and sustainable growth in line with our strategic
               priorities. Ultimately, these results reaffirm the underlying strength and resilience of our franchise.
               I extend my sincere appreciation to our customers, staff, regulators, shareholders and other
               stakeholders for their continued trust and confidence in the Bank.

               With regard to the fraud identified in April 2026, the findings of the final forensic report are
               generally consistent with matters identified through the Bank’s internal investigations. The Bank
               continues to pursue all available avenues for recovery, appropriate legal and internal action, and
               implement relevant controls, technology and governance enhancements, while keeping the CBSL
               updated on the measures being taken.

               While the incident has undoubtedly tested the organisation, our collective response has reinforced
               the strength of our staff and our institution. The commitment, resilience and shared sense of
               purpose demonstrated across the Bank have sharpened our focus on controls, governance and
               operational excellence. It has reaffirmed that the true strength of the franchise lies not only in how
               we perform, but also in how we come together, respond and emerge stronger from adversity.

               Looking ahead, we remain optimistic about the Bank’s prospects. Our sound capital and liquidity
               positions, resilient franchise, talented workforce, technological capabilities and continuously
               strengthening governance and operational frameworks provide a solid platform from which to
               pursue the next phase of our growth.

               We will remain disciplined in managing risk, capital, liquidity and costs to support measured,
               responsible and sustainable growth, while deepening customer relationships, and leveraging
               technology to further enhance customer experience and operating efficiency.

               As opportunities emerge alongside Sri Lanka’s economic progress, we believe the Bank is well
               positioned to support our customers, contribute to the country’s economic advancement and
               translate the underlying strength of our franchise into sustainable growth”.

               4   Expected Credit Losses
                   The Bank assessed the Expected Credit Losses (ECL) of credit facilities based on two approaches of individual and collective basis. The customers who have been
                   identified as individually significant were assessed under individual basis and they were staged based on the identified indicators of increase in credit risks.
                   The Expected Credit Losses assessed on collective impairment approach were based on Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD) and the Economic
                   Factor Adjustment (EFA) by applying the recent forecasts and projections. Further, the Bank has accounted for management overlays to mitigate any expected
                   losses by downgrading the staging of facilities included in the identified risk elevated industries where necessary to capture significant increases in credit risk
                   due to stressed economic conditions.

               5   From total debt investment portfolio ,the Bank has reclassified the Debt portfolio amounting to LKR 37.7 Bn, consisting of Sri Lanka Government Treasury Bonds
                   of LKR 32.6 Bn and Sri Lanka USD Sovereign Bonds of USD 14 Mn held as “Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income” to “Amortised cost” with effect
                   from 01 April 2022. This reclassification is a one-time event in accordance with the Statement of Alternative Treatment (SoAT) on reclassification of debt
                   portfolio issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka and in line with the requirements of the SLFR 9-Financial Instruments paragraph 5.6.5. Sri
                   Lanka USD Sovereign Bonds has been restructured in December 2024.
                   If these investments continued to be carried at fair value through other comprehensive income, the fair value of the remaining portfolio as at 30 September
                   2026 would have amounted to LKR 2.4 Bn and would have resulted in the recognition of fair value loss of LKR 12 Mn in other comprehensive income during the
                   period.
                   Following the improvement in Sri Lanka’s sovereign credit rating, the probability of default (PD) applied in the expected credit loss (ECL) assessment of the
                   Bank’s investment in Sri Lanka International Sovereign (PDI) bonds issued under the sovereign debt restructuring process was recalibrated and adjusted
                   accordingly. The resulting impact was recognized in the financial statements in accordance with SLFRS 9 – Financial Instruments.

                                                                         FAIR VALUE OF FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS

    Fair Values of Financial Instruments are determined according to the following hierarchy as described below:

    Level 1 - quoted market price (unadjusted): financial instruments with quoted prices in active markets
    Level 2 - valuation techniques using observable inputs: financial instruments with quoted prices for similar instruments in active markets or quoted prices for
    identical or similar instruments in inactive markets and financial instruments are valued using models where all significant inputs are observable.
    Level 3 - valuation techniques with significant unobservable inputs: This category includes all instruments valued using valuation techniques where one or more
    significant inputs are unobservable.

    There were no material transfers between levels of fair value hierarchy during the current period of 2026. The Bank did not changed the valuation models and
    assumptions used to measure the fair values of Level 03 financial instruments during the year ended 31 December 2025.
