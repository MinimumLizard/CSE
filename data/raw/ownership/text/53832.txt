NOTES TO THE INTERIM CONDENSED FINANCIAL STATEMENTS

  1    CORPORATE INFORMATION
       Ceylon Beverage Holdings PLC is a public limited liability Company incorporated and domiciled in Sri Lanka and listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange. The parent
       company of Ceylon Beverage Holdings PLC is Carson Cumberbatch PLC "(CCPLC)" and the ultimate parent company is Bukit Darah PLC.
       The interim condensed financial statements comprise of the Company and its subsidiaries (together referred to as the "Group" and individually Group entities).
       The Subsidiaries of the Company are set out below.
       Subsidiary                                 Controlling interest                                                  Note
       Lion Brewery (Ceylon) PLC                       52.25%          "LBCPLC"
       Lion Beer (Ceylon) Pte Ltd, Singapore           52.25%          wholly owned Subsidiary of ''LBCPLC''
       Millers Brewery Limited                         52.25%          wholly owned Subsidiary of ''LBCPLC''
       Pubs 'N Places (Private) Limited                 99.9%
       Retail Spaces (Private) Limited                  100%
       Luxury Brands (Private) Limited                  100%

       The principal activities of the Group is brewing and marketing of high quality beers for both local & export markets and retailing of beer & alcohol products locally
       through its owned/managed wine shops & pubs. The Group is also engaged in the import & marketing of globally renowned high quality beer and spirits brands.

  2    BASIS OF PREPARATION
 2.1   Statement of Compliance
       The interim condensed financial statements of the Group have been prepared in accordance with Sri Lanka Accounting Standard - LKAS 34 (Interim Financial
       Reporting). These interim condensed financial statements should be read in conjunction with the last annual audited financial statements for the year ended 31st
       March 2026, as the same accounting policies have been applied consistently.

 2.2   Going Concern
       Based on currently available information the management is satisfied that Company and its subsidiaries would continue its operations in the foreseeable future.

 2.3 Basis of measurement
2.3.1 - Financial Statements have been prepared on historical cost basis and applied consistently with no adjustments being made for inflationary factors affecting the
      financial statements, except for the following;
        - Freehold land and buildings are measured at cost at the time of acquisition and construction, and subsequently revalued to their fair values as of the date of
       revaluation, less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses, if any.
       - The employee benefit obligation is measured at the present value of the defined benefit obligation at the end of each reporting period. For interim periods, a
       provision is estimated by extrapolating the latest available valuation from the previous reporting period.
       - Investment properties are measured at fair value.

       - Equity investments not held for trading, irrevocably designated at FVOCI on initial recognition. These investments are subsequently measured at fair value, with:
       Other net gains and losses recognised in OCI, which are not reclassified to profit or loss, even upon disposal.
       Instead, cumulative gains or losses will be transferred within equity (e.g., to retained earnings) upon derecognition.

 2.4   Functional Currency and Presentation Currency
       All values presented in the Financial Statements are in Sri Lankan Rupees Thousands (Rs.'000s) which is the Group's functional currency, unless otherwise

        Provision) Duty purportedly in arrears during the period 1998 to 2001 and Rs. 25,059,553.86 as its penalty. The Company and the Directors filed a Writ Application in the Court of Appeal to
        quash the Certificate of Excise Duty in Default and obtained a Stay Order in respect of the Fort MC proceedings, having paid the disputed duty amount of Rs.23,062,080.43 to Sri Lanka
        Customs as required before submitting its appeal. No provision has been made for the penalty of Rs.25,059,553.86.

        The Court of Appeal dismissed the said Writ Application by Judgment dated 18th June 2012. Subsequently, the said Judgment of the Court of Appeal was appealed to the Supreme Court.
        This matter was taken up for a full day of Argument on 12th July 2021. By Judgment dated 16th December 2022, the Supreme Court allowed the Appeal of CBHL and set aside the aforesaid
        Judgment of the Court of Appeal. The Director General of Excise is directed to consider the representations made by the Company in respect of the second show cause notice and make a
        determination under Section 9(2) of the said Act.
        Despite the lapse of several years, an inquiry is still pending. Next steps in the conduct of the inquiry will depend on the determination made by the Director General of Customs and Excise
        Duty and this is being followed up with the relevant Authority as well as through the Attorney General's Department.

14     OTHER INFORMATION

       Registration by Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for VAT on the Supply of Financial Services (VAT ON FS)
       The IRD has forced registered Ceylon Beverage Holdings PLC for VAT on FS on the purported basis that the Company is engaged in the business of
       supplying financial services. The Company has challenged the purported forced registration in the Court of Appeal by filing applications in the nature of
       Writs of Certiorari and Prohibition, on the basis that provisions in the Value Added Tax Act No.14 of 2002 (as amended), pertaining to VAT on FS, are not
       applicable to the Company. On 21st May 2025, CBHL Counsel made submissions insisting that this case be resolved as soon as possible as the IRD has
       continued to issue assessment of VAT for the year 2020/2021 after undertaking to refrain from doing so till the case was concluded. The Respondent
       Counsel informed Court that IRD has undertaken not to issue any Assessments until the conclusion of the case. After several postponements / date
       rescheduling that took place in the latter half of 2025 this case was taken up for Arguments on several court dates in the month of February and March
       2026 wherein both CBHL Counsel and State Counsel presented lengthy Oral Submissions to Court. Both Parties were thereafter directed to file Written
       Submissions, and the case was fixed for Judgment on 21st September 2026. Several postponements of the Judgement have since taken place, and this
       matter is now fixed for Judgement on 20th January 2027.

       Hence, no provision has been made in the Financial Statements, as the matter is presently pending determination by the Court of Appeal.
