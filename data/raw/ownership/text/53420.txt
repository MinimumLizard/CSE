E.B. Creasy & Co. PLC and Group
Performance Review for the Six Months Ended 30th September 2026
Overview

For the six months ended 30th September
2026, the Group delivered revenue of Rs. 17
billion, a 3% decrease YoY. Profit Before Tax
(PBT) stood at Rs. 483 million, while Profit
After Tax (PAT) was recorded at Rs. 265
million for the period.

The Group continued to operate in a
challenging      economic       environment,
characterised by cautious purchasing patterns,
input cost pressures and heightened
competition across key product categories.
Consumers continued to place greater
emphasis on affordability and value,
influencing demand across selected product
segments.
Despite the decline in revenue, the gross profit
margin remained broadly unchanged, supported by proactive pricing measures undertaken to safeguard
margins against potential cost pressures for key products.
The Group declared an interim dividend of Rs. 0.50 per share on 06th November 2026.


Operating Environment
Sri Lanka recorded real economic growth of 4.7% year-on-year in the first half of 2026. However, the
growth of the economy remained vulnerable to inflationary pressures exacerbated by higher energy costs
and external uncertainties.
Headline inflation, measured by the Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), rose from 7.3% in July to
8.0% in August and remained at 8.0% in September. The Central Bank of Sri Lanka maintained the
Overnight Policy Rate at 8.75% in September, following the monetary policy tightening implemented in
May 2026.
The Sri Lankan rupee depreciated by 6.3% against the US dollar on a year-to-date basis at end-September
2026. Global energy-price volatility and geopolitical uncertainty continued to pose risks to inflation,
exchange-rate stability and the broader economic outlook.



                                                   1
For the Group, these conditions, compounded by the Middle East conflict, increased imported input costs
and weighed on consumer demand. Fuel, raw material and packaging costs rose sharply compared with
pre-conflict levels, while pressure on household budgets continued to constrain purchasing power.

The Group continues to manage these pressures through prudent procurement, cost optimization and
carefully calibrated pricing decisions, with a focus on protecting margins while remaining competitive.



Financial and Operating Performance
For the six months ended 30th September 2026, Group revenue declined by 3% YoY to Rs. 17 billion. The
impact on profitability was more pronounced, with Profit Before Tax (PBT) declining by 53% to Rs. 483
million and Profit After Tax (PAT) declining by 61% to Rs. 265 million. The decline in earnings was
primarily driven by higher imported input costs, increased fuel and energy costs.

Revenue of the consumer segment increased marginally by 1% while the industrial segment recorded a
sharp decline of 25% compared to the corresponding period. The Wellness & Nutrition segment recorded
a YoY growth of 14%, bypassing the other two segments.

Notwithstanding the decline in revenue, the Group maintained its gross profit margin broadly in line with
the corresponding period of the previous year.

To mitigate potential supply chain disruptions arising from the conflict in the middle east, the Group
increased inventory holdings by Rs. 3.3 billion compared with 31st March 2026. The resulting increase in
working capital requirements contributed to a Rs. 1.7 billion increase in Group borrowings over the same
period and higher finance costs.


Segmental Performance
Consumer Segment
The Consumer segment remained the Group's principal revenue contributor for the six months ended 30th
September 2026, recording revenue of Rs. 10 billion and a PBT of Rs. 161 million during the period.
Home & Personal Care:
The Home & Personal Care division recorded revenue of Rs. 7.1 billion, an increase of 7.1% compared
with the corresponding period of the previous financial year. Volume growth remained constrained across
categories, reflecting subdued consumer demand amid continued pressure on household purchasing
power.
To address evolving consumer needs, the division introduced value packs and refill pouches across
selected categories to address affordability concerns and sustain volumes. These offerings, together with
new product variants, seasonal promotions and anticipated seasonal demand, are expected to support
volume growth in the coming quarters.

                                                    2
In the previous financial year, the division expanded its home care manufacturing facilities and
strengthened its sales force to support market penetration. These investments form part of the division’s
long-term strategy to increase manufacturing capability and deliver sustainable growth through effective
distribution. Management expects the benefits of the expanded facilities to emerge progressively as
capacity utilization improves. Performance continues to be monitored closely against operational and
financial targets.
Foods:
The Food division recorded revenue of Rs. 2.9 billion, a YoY decline of 11%. Performance during the
period was affected by lower demand from industrial customers for food ingredients, together with supply
constraints arising from the Middle East conflict. The confectionery business demonstrated resilience in
sustaining revenues, although profitability remained challenged by cost escalations.



Industrial Segment

During the period, the Industrial segment recorded revenue of Rs. 3.3 billion and a PBT of Rs. 190 million,
accounting for 19% of total Group revenue.
Energy & Lighting:

The Energy & Lighting division recorded revenue of Rs. 924 million, accounting for 28% of the Industrial
segment's total revenue. The division's performance was negatively impacted by weakness in the
renewable energy solutions business. Policy uncertainty continued to dampen market demand and delay
project implementation. The division expects this business to grow in the future as Sri Lanka catches up
with the electrification trends presently evident in other markets.

Building & Infrastructure Solutions:

The Building & Infrastructure Solutions division recorded revenue of Rs. 2.3 billion, accounting for 72%
of the Industrial segment's total revenue. The division operated against a challenging backdrop in Sri
Lanka's construction industry during the period. Although overall construction activity continued to
expand, supported largely by government-funded road works and post-cyclone reconstruction, this was
offset by subdued demand from other segments of the construction industry.
These conditions directly affected the division's performance. Demand for building & infrastructure
solutions weakened in the retail market. More significantly, delays in the execution of large-scale
industrial projects reduced sales volumes in the project market, which remains the sector's principal source
of revenue. The sector continues to monitor its project pipeline closely and expects activity to improve as
delayed projects resume and cost pressures ease.




                                                     3
Wellness and Nutrition Segment
For the six months ended 30th September 2026, the Wellness and Nutrition segment generated revenue of
Rs. 3.3 billion and a PBT of Rs. 121 million, contributing 20% of the Group revenue. Further, the
segment’s revenue grew by 14% YoY, making it the fastest growing segment of the Group during the
period and outperforming both the Consumer and Industrial segments. This growth was underpinned by
stronger demand for specialized nutrition and adult care products.

The segment's performance highlights the resilience of its product portfolio, which consists mainly of
essential health and nutrition products. These products have been less affected by the pressure on
household spending seen in other parts of the Group. Looking ahead, the segment will continue to focus
on strengthening its brand presence and expanding its distribution reach to sustain this growth momentum
in the coming quarters.


ESG initiatives

During the Period, the Group continued to strengthen the integration of sustainability into its strategic and
operational decision-making. The Group continued to advance initiatives aimed at strengthening climate
resilience, resource efficiency and operational adaptability, while progressively leveraging digitalized
systems and data-driven approaches to enhance sustainability monitoring, visibility and decision-making
across its operations.
These initiatives further support the Group’s preparedness to respond to evolving climate, environmental
and regulatory developments, while enabling the business to identify emerging opportunities, improve
resource productivity and strengthen its resilience over the short, medium and long term.


Strategic Out look
Looking ahead, the Group will remain focused on strengthening its market position, delivering sustainable
revenue growth and safeguarding profitability in an evolving operating environment. Key priorities
include enhancing brand competitiveness, improving distribution effectiveness, maintaining strict cost
discipline and managing working capital efficiently.

While uncertainty persists over geopolitical developments and their potential impact on the domestic
economy, the Group will continue to pursue its strategic priorities, with a clear focus on operational
resilience, financial discipline and long-term value creation for shareholders.
